# Project Charter

STATUS: COMPLETE (Gate 0)

## Identity

- **Title**: THE LAST TENANT
- **Genre**: Adult psychological thriller, with domestic-suspense and
  mystery elements. Not horror, not supernatural, not literary fiction
  first (though it should read as well-made on a sentence level), not
  cozy.
- **Form**: Standalone novel. Written with limited-series adaptation
  potential in mind as a shape (a small, escalating cast; a single
  central location; a structure that could plausibly break into
  episodes) — this is a positioning note about the material, not a claim
  that adaptation, publication success, or any commercial outcome is
  guaranteed. See `originality_policy.md` for the fuller statement on
  not overclaiming.
- **Length target**: ~92,000–96,000 words.
- **Chapter target**: ~46 chapters across three parts (Part I ch. 1–12,
  ~22–25k words; Part II ch. 13–34, ~43–48k words; Part III ch. 35–46,
  ~22–26k words).
- **POV**: Close third person, past tense. ~85–95% Mara Voss. Remaining
  material may include interview transcripts, claim notes, letters,
  building records, edited footage transcripts, continuity files, and
  brief Elena fragments where justified — never as a crutch, never
  overused (see `01_author_bible/narrative_distance.md`).
- **Language**: Native-level American English.

## The one-sentence version

After her estranged younger sister disappears, an insurance
identity-fraud investigator moves into the sister's last known
apartment — where every resident remembers the missing woman as a
different person, and official records insist the apartment has been
vacant for twelve years.

## Why this project exists (the case for the book)

A thriller premise is worth a full novel when the central mechanism
generates more true, dramatically necessary scenes than it resolves, and
when the investigation is inseparable from the investigator's own
unresolved history. THE LAST TENANT is built so that:

- The mystery's engine (seven incompatible-but-genuine accounts of one
  missing woman) cannot be solved by a single interview, search, or
  document — it requires accumulating and cross-referencing an entire
  building's worth of testimony and physical evidence, which structurally
  demands novel length rather than short-story or novella length.
- The investigation is not incidental to the protagonist — Mara's
  professional method (verification over trust) is simultaneously the
  reason she can solve this case and the reason she nearly lost her
  sister before the case began. Every investigative beat is also a
  character beat. This is the project's central discipline; see
  `02_novel_bible/thematic_argument.md`.
- The antagonist force (Palinode) is not a single criminal but a system
  with genuine moral range — it has protected real victims and enabled
  real predators using the same methods — which sustains ambiguity
  through the whole book rather than collapsing into a simple villain
  reveal.

Full interrogation of whether this case holds up is in
`00_project/gate_1_concept_validation.md`. This charter states the
intent; that document tests it.

## Audience & comparable reading experience

Readers of character-driven psychological thrillers with a strong
procedural/investigative backbone and a mystery that rewards close
reading (fair-play clueing, not gotcha reveals). The book should satisfy
readers who want to feel smart for having half-guessed the shape of the
truth, and who want the emotional resolution (the sisters) to land at
least as hard as the plot resolution (Palinode). No specific comparable
titles are named as canon here — genre positioning is refined in
`02_novel_bible/genre_contract.md` and `12_publishing/` once there's a
finished manuscript to position honestly.

## Non-goals

- This is not a series opener. Sequel space is deliberately bounded —
  see `02_novel_bible/sequel_boundaries.md`.
- This is not a romance with a mystery subplot. Any romantic thread is
  subordinate to the sisters' relationship and optional by default (see
  `03_characters/investigative_counterpart.md`).
- This is not a manual. Nothing in this project may function as
  actionable real-world instruction for identity fraud, evading
  detection, or defeating specific financial or insurance controls — see
  the Hard Rules in `CLAUDE.md` and `00_project/originality_policy.md`.
- This is not a vehicle for depicting domestic violence, disability, old
  age, or childhood as shorthand or spectacle. Where the cast touches
  these (Evelyn Price's account of "Lena Hart," Calvin Moss's mobility/
  agoraphobia, Irena Petrov's age, Malik Reed as a child witness), the
  project charter requires specificity and dignity over trope — tracked
  as a standing item in `risk_register.md` and revisited at every
  relevant gate.

## Definition of project success (process-level, not commercial)

Success for this repository's purposes is a manuscript that:

1. Passes every gate's own criteria (not just "exists").
2. Never breaks its own fair-play contract with the reader.
3. Keeps the sisters' relationship as the emotional throughline a reader
   would name first if asked what the book is "about."
4. Contains no fact invented during drafting that contradicts canon
   locked earlier — or, where canon had to change, an explicit, logged
   decision explaining why and what else was updated as a result.
5. Never asserts or implies guaranteed commercial or critical outcomes
   anywhere in its own production materials.

## Roles

This repository is produced by AI drafting sessions under human
direction, with no persistent memory between sessions other than what is
committed to the repository. Every process rule in `CLAUDE.md` and
`production_workflow.md` exists to compensate for that constraint. The
human principal (the repository owner) is the sole authority for
resolving decisions logged as open in `DECISION_LOG.md` and for approving
gate completions before drafting proceeds past them.
