# Risk Register

STATUS: COMPLETE for Gate 0 (initial population); LIVING document —
update whenever a risk is newly identified, its likelihood/impact
changes, or it's mitigated. Do not delete resolved risks; mark them
resolved with the resolving decision or file referenced.

Format per risk: **ID** — description — likelihood (Low/Med/High) —
impact (Low/Med/High) — mitigation / owning gate — status.

## Creative & craft risk

- **R-001** — "Missing sister" and "unreliable multiple accounts of one
  person" are both heavily worked psychological-thriller territory
  (post-*Gone Girl* market saturation); the book risks reading as
  derivative of genre furniture rather than distinct. — Likelihood:
  High (of the *comparison* being made by readers/reviewers) — Impact:
  Med — Mitigation: the differentiator is mechanism, not premise —
  identity engineering with genuine, non-contradictory multi-witness
  evidence is a structurally different engine than an unreliable-narrator
  or single-liar-exposed structure. Must stay disciplined about this in
  execution (Gate 3). — Status: open, owned by Gate 3.
- **R-002** — The "professionally-perfect-for-this-case" protagonist
  (an identity-fraud investigator whose sister vanishes into an
  identity-fraud scheme) risks reading as convenient rather than ironic/
  thematic. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med — Mitigation: the
  irony has to be *earned* and *costly* — Mara's skill causing the
  problem (her own manufactured childhood identity) rather than only
  solving it is the intended answer; must land clearly, not just be
  assertable. — Status: open, tracked through
  `02_novel_bible/thematic_argument.md` and Gate 3.
- **R-003** — "Sympathetic villain organization" (Palinode having
  protected real victims) is a familiar gray-morality move in modern
  thrillers and risks feeling asserted rather than demonstrated. —
  Likelihood: Med — Impact: High (this is load-bearing for the whole
  book's moral question) — Mitigation: requires at least one fully
  realized, on-page case of Palinode protecting someone legitimately, not
  just Julian Vale's say-so — a Gate 2/4 requirement, not just a Gate 1
  premise note. — Status: open.
- **R-004** — Elderly witness (Irena Petrov) and child witness (Malik
  Reed) are both stock supporting-character types in mystery fiction;
  risk of tokenism or "wisdom/innocence" shorthand instead of full
  characters. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med — Mitigation: Gate 2
  stereotype-risk review is mandatory for both, specifically checking
  that each has a want/fear/contradiction independent of their plot
  function. — Status: open, owned by Gate 2.
- **R-005** — The brief's suggested ending image ("You were the first
  tenant") is a strong line but structurally close to a familiar
  thriller-ending device (epistolary gut-punch reveal). — Likelihood:
  Med — Impact: Low (it's one candidate among several, not locked) —
  Mitigation: `04_plot/ending_options.md` treats it as one of four
  compared architectures, not a foregone conclusion. — Status: tracked
  in Gate 1 output; final call deferred to Gate 4.

## Plausibility risk

- **R-006** — Jurisdictional plausibility: an insurance SIU investigator
  has no formal authority over a missing-persons case, which is NYPD
  territory. If the manuscript lets Mara operate as a de facto lead
  investigator without friction, it will read as implausible to any
  reader who knows how this actually works. — Likelihood: High (if
  unaddressed) — Impact: High — Mitigation: this must become a *feature*,
  not a bug — Mara working around, alongside, or against actual police
  process, with real procedural friction, is more interesting than her
  having unearned authority. Needs real research
  (`09_research/FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md`) and an explicit structural answer
  in Gate 3/4. — Status: open, high priority.
- **R-007** — A twelve-year "vacant" apartment in a real-NYC-like
  building would ordinarily draw scrutiny (property tax assessment,
  rent-stabilization audit exposure, a landlord's basic financial
  incentive to lease a unit). Needs a specific, plausible mechanism, not
  just narrative convenience. — Likelihood: High (if unaddressed) —
  Impact: High — Mitigation: addressed at the concept level in Gate 1's
  three compared Apartment 4C explanations; the selected explanation
  must survive a "why hasn't ordinary NYC bureaucracy caught this"
  question specifically. — Status: addressed at concept level in
  `gate_1_concept_validation.md`; needs real-estate/tax research to
  fully ground (queued).
- **R-008** — Analog-only evidence (paper maintenance logs, physical
  photographs) surviving as load-bearing plot evidence risks feeling
  anachronistic against how thoroughly digital most record-keeping
  actually is in 2020s NYC. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med —
  Mitigation: Gabriel Knox's analog record-keeping must be characterized
  as a *deliberate choice* (avoiding discoverability) rather than
  unexplained old-fashionedness — see `03_characters/gabriel_knox.md`
  and `02_novel_bible/bellwether_house.md`. — Status: addressed at
  concept level; needs consistency check at Gate 3.
- **R-009** — Palinode's decades-long operational security (per Gate 1,
  it relies on human dependency, not magic) still needs a concrete answer
  for why *this specific case* (Elena's investigation) is the one that
  breaks it, rather than any of presumably many prior cases over decades.
  — Likelihood: Med — Impact: High — Mitigation: must be answered
  specifically, not left implicit — see the "why is Mara dangerous to
  Palinode" question in `gate_1_concept_validation.md`. — Status: open,
  owned by Gate 3.

## Emotional risk

- **R-010** — The six-timeline, twelve-character mystery machinery is
  substantial enough to structurally crowd out the sisters' relationship
  as the book's emotional center, which the source brief explicitly
  identifies as the non-negotiable core. — Likelihood: High (mechanism
  risk is inherent to a book this plotted) — Impact: High — Mitigation:
  `04_plot/emotional_curve.md` (Gate 4) must be checked chapter-by-chapter
  against `tension_curve.md`, specifically flagging any stretch where
  plot machinery advances for multiple chapters with no sisters-relevant
  beat. This is the single risk this whole project charter treats as
  most important to actively manage, not just note. — Status: open,
  standing item for every future gate and, later, every combined chapter
  audit.
- **R-011** — Elena, absent for most of the book by design, risks being
  unable to justify 40+ chapters of buildup once she's actually on the
  page — a common failure mode in missing-person structures. —
  Likelihood: Med — Impact: High — Mitigation: Elena must be
  characterized with real flaws and agency *before* she appears
  (`03_characters/elena_voss.md` explicitly requires this — "must not be
  idealized merely because she is missing"), so her eventual appearance
  confirms a complex person rather than a symbol. — Status: open, owned
  by Gate 2.
- **R-012** — Mara's concealed statement edit (her "secret") risks
  either landing too early (undercutting sympathy before the reader is
  invested) or too late (feeling like a cheap last-act twist rather than
  a seeded flaw). — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med — Mitigation: explicit
  placement decision required at Gate 4 (`reveal_ladder.md`), not left to
  drafting instinct. — Status: open.

## Market positioning risk

- **R-013** — Title uniqueness: "THE LAST TENANT" is a common-enough
  phrase structure in the thriller/domestic-suspense space that it is
  very likely not unique in the marketplace (this repository's own prior
  rejected branch used the identical title, and a related manuscript by
  this same author used the near-identical "THE SILENT TENANT" —
  see `DECISION_LOG.md`). Not a legal problem (titles generally aren't
  copyrightable), but a real discoverability/positioning problem. —
  Likelihood: High — Impact: Low–Med — Mitigation: flagged in
  `gate_1_concept_validation.md`'s title stress-test; a marketplace title
  search is recommended before any public-facing use, tracked in
  `12_publishing/title_review.md`. — Status: open, low urgency until
  publishing phase.
- **R-014** — Crowded comp space generally (see R-001) is also a market
  risk, not just a craft risk — agents/editors may pattern-match quickly
  to saturated territory. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med — Mitigation:
  differentiation messaging deferred to `12_publishing/` once there's a
  finished manuscript to position honestly; not a drafting-phase concern.
  — Status: deferred.

## Legal & sensitivity risk

- **R-015** — Operational harm: any depiction of Palinode's methods that
  crosses from "plausible thriller texture" into "usable real-world
  how-to" for identity fraud. — Likelihood: Low if actively guarded,
  Med if not — Impact: High — Mitigation: `originality_policy.md` §4 is
  the standing rule; every Palinode-methods scene gets checked against it
  at drafting time. — Status: open, standing guard rail for all future
  work, not a one-time check.
- **R-016** — Domestic-violence relocation storyline (Evelyn Price's
  account of "Lena Hart") risks misrepresenting how DV relocation and
  protective services actually work if under-researched, which could
  read as either inaccurate or (worse) inadvertently unsafe-sounding
  advice to a reader in a similar situation. — Likelihood: Med — Impact:
  High — Mitigation: dedicated research required
  (`09_research/FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md`); a sensitivity read is recommended
  before this storyline is drafted, not just before publication. —
  Status: open, high priority for Gate 2/3.
- **R-017** — A minor character (Malik Reed, 12–14) exists in proximity
  to a missing-person/criminal-network narrative. Risk of the child being
  used as a danger-prop or an implausibly all-knowing plot device rather
  than a credible kid. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: Med — Mitigation:
  explicitly required by the character brief itself ("must be a credible
  child, not an all-knowing plot device"); Gate 2 stereotype-risk review
  must confirm this in the finished character file, and no drafted scene
  may place Malik in direct physical danger from Palinode without a
  specific, deliberate story reason logged in `DECISION_LOG.md`. —
  Status: open.
- **R-018** — Depicting a fictional insurance company's internal fraud
  practices, and a fictional NYC building's ownership structure, in ways
  that could be misread as claims about real institutions. —
  Likelihood: Low — Impact: Low — Mitigation: fictionalization is
  already policy (`originality_policy.md`, `CLAUDE.md` hard rules); no
  real company or building names anywhere in the project. — Status:
  standing guard rail, low current concern.

## Process & continuity risk

- **R-019** — No persistent memory between sessions is the single
  largest structural risk to a 46-chapter, multi-session project — any
  process failure here compounds across the whole manuscript. —
  Likelihood: High if unmanaged — Impact: High — Mitigation: this is
  the entire reason the gate system, registers, and mandatory session
  handoff files (`PROJECT_STATUS.md`, `NEXT_ACTION.md`, `DECISION_LOG.md`)
  exist. — Status: standing, actively mitigated by
  `production_workflow.md`, not a one-time fix.
- **R-020** — Six-timeline mystery tracking (`05_mystery/`) is complex
  enough that internal contradiction between timelines is a realistic
  risk as more chapters accumulate. — Likelihood: Med — Impact: High —
  Mitigation: Gate 3's mandatory cross-timeline consistency check, plus
  periodic combined audits per `production_workflow.md`. — Status: open,
  owned by Gate 3 and ongoing thereafter.
- **R-021** — Scale risk: with ~46 chapters and no single session likely
  to draft the whole book, cross-chapter drift (names, dates, physical
  details, voice) is a near-certainty without discipline. — Likelihood:
  High — Impact: Med — Mitigation: `08_continuity/` registers plus
  mandatory combined audits at every part boundary (and recommended every
  4–6 chapters) per `production_workflow.md`. — Status: standing,
  infrastructure in place, effectiveness depends on future sessions
  actually using it.
