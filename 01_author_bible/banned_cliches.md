# Banned Clichés

STATUS: DRAFT — living list. Add to this file the moment a future
drafting session catches a new instance of flat, over-familiar phrasing
or a stock beat — don't wait for a dedicated audit pass to log it.

## Genre-wide stock phrases (banned outright)

- "Little did she know"
- "Something told her"
- "A chill ran down her spine" / any variant chill-down-the-spine
  construction
- "She let out a breath she didn't know she was holding"
- "Time seemed to stand still" / "time seemed to slow"
- "Her blood ran cold"
- "A sense of unease settled over her"
- "Deep down, she knew"
- "The silence was deafening"
- Any construction pairing a body-horror-adjacent physical reaction
  (stomach dropping, world tilting, ears ringing) with a plot revelation,
  used more than once in the entire manuscript — one instance,
  well-placed, is a choice; a repeated pattern is a tic.

## Stock beats (banned as written, not as concepts)

- The unidentified-woman-in-danger cold open (explicitly excluded from
  the approved opening options — see `04_plot/opening_options.md`).
- The villain monologue that explains the whole scheme unprompted.
- The "44-hour wait" missing-persons myth (NYPD does not, in current
  real practice, require a fixed waiting period before accepting a
  missing-adult report — using this myth uncritically would be both a
  factual error and a cliché; verify current real practice at Gate 3 via
  `09_research/FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md` before any scene relies on it).
- The love-interest-as-reward structural beat (this book defaults to no
  conventional romance — see `03_characters/investigative_counterpart.md`
  — and any romantic thread that does exist may not resolve as a "prize"
  for solving the case).
- A killer/antagonist who is omniscient or unstoppable purely for
  tension — banned by `02_novel_bible/palinode_system.md`'s realism
  constraints, repeated here because it's also a craft cliché, not just
  a plausibility problem.
- The "gotcha" reveal that depends on the reader having been actively
  misled by the narration rather than under-informed by the plot's
  natural fog — see `prose_style_guide.md`'s fair-play rule.

## Project-specific: do not converge on the rejected prior branch

Per `00_project/originality_policy.md`, the following specific
executions are banned in this project because they belong to the
rejected `claude/last-tenant-part-one-ch1-4-nfwdvx` branch's material —
not generic genre clichés, but this-repository-specific reuse risks:

- A taciturn building caretaker/superintendent whose "tell" is verbally
  saying more than he means to and then immediately regretting it and
  retreating into small talk. (Gabriel Knox needs a different behavioral
  signature — see `03_characters/gabriel_knox.md` once built at Gate 2.)
- A buzzer/directory panel with a visible "ghost" of a previous
  occupant's label under a new one, used as a meaningful early clue.
  (Already caught once and corrected in `02_novel_bible/bellwether_house.md`
  — replaced with the inconsistent-numbering-scheme device instead.)
- An ambient building sound (a mechanical clank, a specific creak) used
  as recurring chapter-ending punctuation.
- The narrating character's habitual mental vocabulary for
  noticing-but-not-saying being verb-anchored on "filing" or "flagging."
- A single hidden physical cavity/patch in a wall functioning as the
  central physical-evidence mechanism the whole mystery pivots on.
- A landlord-or-owner-figure whose menace is primarily expressed through
  excessive personal hospitality/attentiveness (hosting dinners,
  remembering personal details unprompted, physically taking control of
  small tasks). Julian Vale's menace should be ideological and
  systemic, not a performance of over-familiar warmth in social scenes.

If a future session notices a new beat converging on that branch's
material — even one not listed above — add it to this section
immediately, the same session it's noticed.

## Enforcement

Every chapter's Prose Audit (once drafting begins, per
`00_project/production_workflow.md`) checks the drafted text against
this file specifically, in addition to general line-level craft. A match
against anything on this list is a required revision, not a style
suggestion.
