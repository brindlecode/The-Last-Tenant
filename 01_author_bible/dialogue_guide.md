# Dialogue Guide

STATUS: DRAFT — Gate 1 level; will be stress-tested against real
character voices once Gate 2 fully builds each character's speech
pattern and private vocabulary, and again once sample chapters exist.

## Every line of dialogue must

- **Contain a goal.** A character says something because they want
  something from the exchange — information, reassurance, distance,
  control, relief. If a line of dialogue exists only to convey
  information to the reader with no in-scene want behind it, rewrite the
  scene or cut the line.
- **Carry subtext.** What a character says and what they mean should not
  always be identical — especially for Mara (who under-states emotion by
  professional habit) and Julian Vale (whose warmth and reasonableness
  are real and simultaneously a control mechanism — see
  `03_characters/julian_vale.md`).
- **Differ by character.** Each named character needs a distinguishable
  speech pattern — not a gimmick or verbal tic used as a crutch, but a
  real difference in rhythm, vocabulary, directness, and what they avoid
  saying. Full per-character notes belong in each character's file
  (`03_characters/`) once Gate 2 builds them out; this file governs the
  cross-cutting rules.
- **Include natural misunderstanding.** People in this book, especially
  the sisters, frequently mishear each other's intent even when the
  words are clear — that's the book's central relationship dynamic
  (`02_novel_bible/thematic_argument.md`) and should show up at the
  sentence level, not just the plot level.
- **Avoid exposition dumps.** No character explains something to another
  character who would already know it, purely so the reader can
  overhear the explanation. Where real exposition is unavoidable (e.g.,
  Palinode's methods being explained to Mara by someone who does know
  more than she does), it must be resisted, interrupted, or paid for by
  the character delivering it — information should cost something to
  extract, not arrive in a clean paragraph.
- **Use contractions naturally**, the way real American speech does.
  Formal, contraction-free dialogue reads as stiff unless a specific
  character's precision is the point (a candidate trait for Mara under
  stress, used deliberately and sparingly, not as her default register).

## Per-relationship dialogue notes (expand at Gate 2)

- **Mara & Elena**: the most important dialogue relationship in the
  book. Their exchanges (live, remembered, or via the voicemail/
  fragments) should demonstrate the "unequal memory" theme directly —
  each sister should occasionally reference a shared past moment in a
  way that reveals they remember it differently, without either being
  flagged as wrong.
- **Mara & Julian Vale**: his dialogue should never feel like villain
  speechifying. He is reasonable, warm, and precise, and his most
  unsettling lines should be the ones a reasonable person could almost
  agree with. Avoid any dialogue beat that reads as him gloating,
  monologuing his master plan, or being cartoonishly cold — that would
  break `02_novel_bible/palinode_system.md`'s realism constraints.
- **Mara & Det. Teo Marchetti**: professional friction rendered through
  procedure-literate dialogue (real jurisdictional/process language,
  used correctly — flag for research verification, not invented
  casually) rather than generic "you're not a cop" antagonism.
- **The seven witnesses**: each should have a speech pattern that
  reflects their profession/background (Evelyn Price's clinical nurse's
  precision about physical detail; Jonah Bell's lawyerly hedging; Rina
  Cho's editor's eye for sequence and framing; Calvin Moss's specialist
  vocabulary around data; Malik Reed sounding like an actual
  twelve-to-fourteen-year-old, not a small adult) — this is as much a
  stereotype-risk safeguard as a craft one; see `risk_register.md`
  R-004.

## Banned dialogue moves

- Any character explaining their own psychology in clinical/therapy
  language (see `prose_style_guide.md`).
- Aphorism-heavy dialogue for any character (reserve genuine quotable
  lines for rare, earned moments, never as a default register).
- "As you know" exposition between characters who both already know.
- Dialogue used to withhold a fact the speaking character has no
  in-scene reason to withhold, purely for reader suspense (distinct from
  a character who has a real, motivated reason to withhold — that's
  fine and often necessary).
