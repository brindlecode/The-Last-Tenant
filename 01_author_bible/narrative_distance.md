# Narrative Distance

STATUS: DRAFT — Gate 1 level. This file exists because "close third
person" is a rule people think they're following right up until they
aren't; it needs mechanical, checkable criteria, not just a label.

## The core rule

Everything in the narration is filtered through what Mara can perceive,
infer, remember, or be told. The prose may render her *inferences* about
other people vividly and confidently — that's normal close-third
technique, and flat, hedge-everything prose ("he seemed to maybe be
frustrated, possibly") is its own craft failure. The line is between
**Mara's read of someone, rendered as her read**, and **a fact about
someone else's actual interior state, rendered as narrated truth**.

### Acceptable (Mara's inference, even unhedged in phrasing)

"He had the specific patience of a man used to being believed." — This
is Mara characterizing what she observes; a confident-sounding sentence
is fine as long as it's legible as *her read*, not an omniscient claim.
Confidence of tone is not the same as head-hopping — a strongly-voiced
inference is still an inference if the content is something Mara could
plausibly conclude from behavior, not something only accessible from
inside the other person's head.

### Not acceptable (stated as narrated fact, not inference)

"He had decided years ago that warmth was the most efficient tool he
owned." — This asserts a specific, private, historical decision inside
someone else's mind as if the narration simply knows it. No amount of
confident phrasing makes this Mara's inference; it's an interiority
claim about someone else, which close third person filtered through
Mara cannot make. If a chapter needs this kind of insight about, say,
Julian Vale, it has to arrive through what he says, does, or is
reported to have done — never asserted directly.

**Test**: could Mara plausibly have arrived at this specific claim by
watching, listening, or being told — or does it require access to a
private, undisclosed fact about someone else's inner life? If the
answer is the latter, it's a violation regardless of how well-written the
sentence is. This exact failure mode (an ungrounded interiority claim
about a non-POV character, phrased confidently enough to read as
narrated fact) is a known, previously-occurring error in this project's
production history — see `DECISION_LOG.md` for the standing note to
watch for it specifically during every future Voice Audit.

## Julian Vale specifically

Per `02_novel_bible/palinode_system.md` and `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`,
Julian's interiority is never accessed, full stop — treat this as the
single strictest instance of the rule above, the same way the project
this repository's methodology was adapted from treated its own primary
antagonist. Every insight into Julian must arrive through observable
behavior, his own words, or what other characters report.

## Alternative materials (documents, transcripts, fragments)

The ~5–15% of the book that isn't Mara's close-third narration exists to
do something dramatized scene-narration can't — show a piece of Elena's
own voice directly, show how a manufactured record is *constructed* as
an artifact, or compress information that would otherwise require an
inert scene of Mara reading something at length. Use only where the
chapter brief specifically justifies it. A document insert used to avoid
writing a hard scene is a crutch, not a technique — the Voice and Pacing
Audits (once drafting begins) should treat an overused document insert as
a finding, not a stylistic choice beyond question.

## Elena fragments specifically

"Brief Elena fragments only when justified" (source brief's own
language). Working rule: an Elena fragment earns its place when it gives
the reader something Mara's own investigation genuinely could not have
reconstructed — Elena's actual interior experience of a moment, not a
summary of plot information Mara will learn anyway through other means.
If a planned Elena fragment could be cut without losing anything the
reader needs that isn't available elsewhere, cut it.
