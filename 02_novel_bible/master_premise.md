## Logline

After her estranged younger sister disappears, an insurance identity-
fraud investigator moves into the sister's last known apartment. Every
resident remembers the missing woman under a different name, profession,
history, and personality, while official records insist the apartment
has been vacant for twelve years.

## Expanded premise

Mara Voss is a senior investigator specializing in complex identity
fraud — staged deaths, fabricated claims, manipulated records, false
relationships, synthetic identities, invented personal histories — for a
major insurance company's Special Investigations Unit.

Her younger sister, Elena Voss, disappears after leaving Mara a
fragmented voicemail Mara initially dismisses as one more instance of
Elena's secrecy and instability — a judgment consistent with a decade of
prior instances, and consistent with the specific way Mara has always
managed her relationship with Elena: verification before belief.

Elena's last confirmed trace is Apartment 4C, Bellwether House, a
fictional residential building in Brooklyn. Management records state the
unit has been legally vacant for twelve years. Seven residents
nonetheless remember a woman matching Elena's description as a real,
specific presence in their lives — under seven different names, with
seven different professions, personalities, and personal histories, each
account carrying its own genuine, non-fabricated evidence. No single
account is a lie. They still cannot all describe one ordinary life.

Mara's investigation surfaces a private identity-engineering network —
the Palinode Continuity Group — that has spent decades manufacturing
complete, socially defensible lives for people who need to disappear.
Palinode's innovation is not forged documents; it's manufactured
*continuity*: neighbors who genuinely remember a person, photographs,
rental histories, employment references, routines, medical traces,
correspondence, witnesses, entire emotional histories, built to survive
scrutiny because the people inside them are real and the relationships
are, in their way, real too.

Elena entered Bellwether House not primarily to disappear, but to
investigate the manufactured origin of Mara's own identity — and, it
emerges, the origin of their shared childhood. Mara discovers that her
own professional gift — for detecting exactly this kind of manufactured
continuity — exists because she has spent her career recognizing, in
other people's cases, the method that was once used on her own family.

## Central dramatic question

What makes a person real: their body, their records, their memories, the
people who recognize them, or the story they choose to live by?

## The book's actual subject (do not lose this while building plot)

This is, first, the story of two sisters who loved each other badly.
Mara treated facts as more trustworthy than Elena. Elena experienced
Mara's demand for proof as a refusal of love. The investigation is not
only a search for a missing person — it is a forced reckoning with every
moment Elena asked to be believed and Mara chose verification instead.
See `thematic_argument.md` for full development; see `risk_register.md`
R-010 for the standing risk that plot machinery crowds this out, which
every future gate must actively guard against.
