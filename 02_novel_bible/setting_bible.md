# Setting Bible

STATUS: Gate 1 COMPLETE at this level; fine-grained neighborhood texture
(specific streets, businesses, transit) built out as needed during Gate
4/drafting, following the rules below.

## Baseline

Contemporary New York City. Real city, real boroughs, real general
geography and institutions (the existence of NYPD, FDNY, the MTA, HPD/DOB
as agency types) may be referenced generically. **No real specific
address, real specific named business, real specific named building, or
real named private individual is ever used.** Where the story needs a
business (a bar, a diner, a records-storage company, a title/escrow
firm), invent one; where it needs an agency-level institutional fact
(e.g., "HPD handles housing code enforcement"), that level of general,
public, structural fact is fine to reference by its real name — the line
is between citing how *public institutions generically function* (fine,
often necessary for plausibility) and inventing or implying facts about
*specific real private entities or addresses* (never fine).

## Brooklyn

Bellwether House sits on a fictional street — Bellwether Street — placed,
for internal geographic consistency, in the general vicinity of
brownstone/pre-war central Brooklyn (the belt that runs roughly through
Fort Greene, Clinton Hill, and Prospect Heights) without being pinned to
one single real, identifiable block. This gives the neighborhood texture
real specificity requires (transit options, gentrification pressure,
building stock era, demographic mix) while keeping the building and its
immediate streetscape fully invented. Future sessions building out
`08_continuity/locations.md` should keep new invented streets,
businesses, and cross-streets consistent with this general placement
(walkable to a real-feeling commercial strip, near but not on top of a
subway line, a genuine mix of pre-war rental buildings, brownstones, and
newer infill) without ever claiming a specific real intersection.

## Time period

Present-day, contemporary, unpinned to a specific real year (avoid
dating the book to real datable news events; reference technology and
culture at a generic "now" level rather than anything that will read as
dated in five years where avoidable).

## Neighborhood texture rules

- Socially diverse, multi-generational, genuinely mixed-income within the
  same building (matches Bellwether House's cast: a retired nurse, a
  family-law attorney, a documentary editor, a data broker, a
  12–14-year-old and his family, an 80-something long-term resident) —
  this diversity should read as an ordinary fact of city apartment living,
  not as a curated "diverse cast" checklist.
- Gentrification pressure is present and textured (a building superintendent
  under pressure to maximize returns; residents at different points in
  the neighborhood's economic change) but is *context*, not a subplot
  competing with the main mystery, unless a specific chapter needs it
  for a specific reason.
- Avoid over-familiar "gritty New York" or "glossy New York" shorthand in
  either direction — the prose should render the city the way a resident
  who's stopped noticing the skyline would, at street and building level
  (see `01_author_bible/prose_style_guide.md`).

## Mara's other primary locations

- **Her employer** (insurance company Special Investigations Unit) —
  fictional company, name TBD at Gate 2 (flagged in
  `03_characters/supporting_cast.md` and `08_continuity/open_questions.md`),
  Manhattan or downtown Brooklyn office, generically upper-mid-size
  corporate insurer, not modeled on any specific real insurer.
- **Mara's own apartment**, pre-investigation — to be built at Gate 2/4,
  should read as competent-but-guarded, similar to her personality; a
  useful contrast location to Bellwether House once she moves in there.
- **NYPD contact points** — a real-feeling but unnamed-specific precinct
  serving the Bellwether House area, where Detective Teo Marchetti is
  based.

## What this setting is not

Not a supernatural or gothic setting (per `CLAUDE.md` and the source
brief) — Bellwether House's uncanny quality comes entirely from human
systems (identity engineering) misusing an ordinary building, never from
the building itself having any inexplicable properties. Not a
science-fiction setting — no technology in this book exceeds real,
current, plausible capability (see `palinode_system.md`'s realism
constraints).
