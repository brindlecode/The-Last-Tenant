# Symbolism Map

STATUS: DRAFT — Gate 1 level, seed material for future drafting. Use
deliberately, per `01_author_bible/prose_style_guide.md`'s eventual
motif-discipline rules — a symbol used in every chapter stops being a
symbol and becomes a tic. Checked against
`00_project/originality_policy.md` specifically to avoid convergence
with the rejected prior branch's motif set (doors/self-closers as
honesty-markers, "filing away" as a noticing-vocabulary, an ambient
building sound as chapter-ending punctuation, a hidden physical
cavity/patch as the central clue mechanism) — none of the symbols below
should be executed the way that material was.

## Tenancy itself

The book's master metaphor, already load-bearing at the title/premise
level: a tenant occupies a space they don't own, under terms that can be
revoked, leaving a record that outlasts the person. Applied to identity
throughout — everyone in this book is, in some sense, a tenant of a life,
including Mara in her own family's manufactured history. Use sparingly at
the explicit, stated-metaphor level (once or twice in the whole book,
maximum); let it work implicitly everywhere else through the plot's own
mechanics rather than through repeated lines calling attention to it.

## Paper versus memory

Two kinds of evidence recur throughout: the document (lease, form,
photograph, record) and the testimony (a neighbor's memory, a felt
relationship). Neither is presented as more trustworthy than the other —
the book's whole architecture depends on both being partially, genuinely
true at once. Visual/textural shorthand: paper is precise and cold and
can still lie by omission; memory is warm and imprecise and can still be
exactly right. Avoid making this a simple "documents lie, people's
hearts know the truth" theme — that's a simpler, less interesting book
than the one this bible describes (see `thematic_argument.md`'s "what
the book is not saying").

## Doubling and multiplicity

Seven accounts of one woman; two sisters who are, in different ways,
both experts in identity (Mara through records, Elena through
performance); a mother who was, herself, once someone else. Use the
number seven deliberately where it appears (seven witnesses is already
locked) but don't force numerological cuteness elsewhere — this is a
structural motif, not a puzzle-box one.

## Access and keys

Who can get into which room, whose name is on which lease, who has a
key versus who has been let in — recurs naturally given the building
setting and the identity-as-occupancy metaphor. A good source of small,
concrete scene-level tension (a locked storage cage, a super's master
key, an old service-stairwell door) without needing to be stated as
symbolic on the page.

## Mail and correspondence

Elena's own communication style (the fragmented voicemail; whatever
written trail she leaves) versus the formal correspondence Palinode's
methods depend on (references, employment letters, medical
correspondence) — the gap between how the sisters actually talk to each
other and how a manufactured identity is built to look like it
communicates is a rich, book-specific vein, distinct from anything in
the rejected prior branch's motif set.

## What NOT to reach for

Explicitly avoided as symbol vocabulary, precisely because of proximity
to the rejected branch's executed motifs: door/lock mechanics as an
honesty-barometer; an ambient environmental sound used as structural
chapter-ending punctuation; the vocabulary of "filing/flagging" for a
character's noticing-versus-saying habit; a single hidden physical
cavity as the central physical-evidence mechanism. This book's
equivalent tools are the ones above — records versus memory, access and
keys, doubling — which arise from *this* premise rather than being
carried over.
