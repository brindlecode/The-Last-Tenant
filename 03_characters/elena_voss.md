# Elena Voss

STATUS: DRAFT — strong seed from source brief plus this session's
invention; **Gate 2 review not yet run.** Per the source brief's own
explicit instruction, repeated here as a standing constraint on every
future draft: Elena must not be idealized merely because she is
missing. She made serious mistakes, deceived Mara, and endangered
others. She remains morally complex through the entire book, including
after she's found.

- **Full name**: Elena Voss. Same manufactured-surname situation as
  Mara — see `mara_voss.md`.
- **Age**: 32.
- **Role**: Missing sister; the book's emotional and investigative
  engine, present mostly through absence, testimony, and fragments for
  most of the novel.
- **Occupation**: Trained in theater (conservatory or drama-school
  background, not a traditional four-year path — an early point of
  quiet family friction with Mara's more conventional route, an early
  version of their whole dynamic). Worked inconsistently as a working
  actor — small roles, voice work, background work — before being drawn
  into paid identity-construction work for Palinode during a lean
  period, initially as what amounts to freelance performance work
  (inhabiting a persona for a client's benefit) before understanding the
  full scope of what she'd joined.
- **Physical presence**: Mobile, adaptable, expressive where Mara is
  still — the physical opposite of her sister by design. Can and does
  visibly shift posture, gesture, and vocal register between contexts, a
  trained skill that is also, to anyone who knows her well, a visible
  tell in itself: people who love her can sometimes see her "put on" a
  different bearing even in ordinary, low-stakes moments, which is its
  own quiet sadness.
- **Family and cultural background**: Shares Mara's manufactured "Voss"
  background; see `mara_voss.md`. Unlike Mara, Elena has — by the time
  the book begins — worked out significantly more of the truth about
  their real origin, which is the entire engine of why she's at
  Bellwether House.
- **Education**: Conservatory/theater training rather than a
  conventional degree; unnamed specific institution (avoid a real named
  school).
- **Economic situation**: Precarious, freelance, gig-to-gig — genuine
  financial vulnerability is part of why Palinode's paid work was
  initially appealing, not just ideological alignment.
- **Living conditions**: Unstable even before her final disappearance —
  moved frequently, kept few permanent attachments to places, which
  reads, in hindsight, as someone who had already spent years
  practicing not being findable.
- **Habits**: Keeps working notebooks in different registers/voices for
  different assumed identities, a practice habit carried over from
  actor training. Picks up other people's speech patterns quickly,
  sometimes without meaning to.
- **Speech pattern**: Adaptable and code-switching by trade; her own
  unperformed voice (rare on the page, valuable when it appears) runs
  quick, defended by wit — she tends to wrap a true, vulnerable thing
  inside a joke specifically so it can be safely denied if it lands
  wrong.
- **Private vocabulary**: Theater-craft language bleeding into ordinary
  speech — "blocking," "a beat," "staying in it" — used naturally enough
  that it becomes, potentially, a fair-play clue: an account of "her"
  from a witness who reports a civilian-implausible turn of phrase is
  worth Mara's attention. Flagged for Gate 3 clue-register use.
- **Contradictions**: Extraordinarily good at making near-strangers feel
  deeply, genuinely known — professionally skilled at exactly the
  intimacy she withholds from the one person who most wants it from her,
  her own sister.
- **Desire (surface want)**: Proof of what happened to their family and
  to Mara's true origin. To be believed by Mara without having to prove
  herself first.
- **Need (what must change for real growth)**: To let herself be known,
  unperformed, by at least one person — specifically Mara, specifically
  by the book's end.
- **Fear**: That if Mara ever saw the real, messy, morally compromised
  version of her, she'd meet the exact same disbelief Mara already
  extends to her "unstable" moments — so she performs a manageable
  version of herself pre-emptively rather than risk the real one being
  rejected.
- **Shame**: Her own complicity in Palinode's harms as an adult
  operative — identities she helped build were later used to hurt
  people, not always with her full knowledge at the time, and at least
  once with knowledge she chose not to act on until later.
- **Secret**: The full extent of her Palinode complicity, and the
  specific trigger that turned her from operative to whistleblower-in-
  progress — not yet fully built; a Gate 3 item.
- **False belief**: "If Mara really loved me, she wouldn't need proof." —
  the precise mirror of Mara's false belief; neither sister is right,
  which is the point (see `02_novel_bible/thematic_argument.md`).
- **Relationship to Mara**: Older-sister-dynamic reversed by role —
  Elena is younger but has always been the one who understood people,
  Mara the one who understood systems. Full treatment in
  `relationship_matrix.md`.
- **Beginning knowledge (at the point the story's action begins)**:
  Understands Palinode's structure well; has assembled partial proof of
  Mara's manufactured origin; is close enough to a dangerous discovery
  at Bellwether House to have become a genuine threat to the
  organization.
- **Ending knowledge**: TBD at Gate 3 — likely includes a fuller truth
  about their father's fate and about Julian Vale's actual regard for
  Mara, both currently open items (see
  `00_project/gate_1_concept_validation.md` Part B3).
- **Lie (ongoing behavior toward Mara, distinct from the Palinode
  secret)**: Never explains to Mara the real reason for her
  secretiveness and instability over the years — lets Mara believe it's
  just "an Elena thing" rather than protective evasion.
- **Reason for the lie**: Partly protective of Mara (genuine danger in
  Mara knowing), partly self-protective (fear of the rejection above),
  partly practical (explaining any of it credibly to a professional
  fraud investigator is its own risk).
- **Behavioral tells**: Becomes *more* performed, brighter, more
  charming, precisely when she is most frightened — the inverse of the
  expected tell, and a specific, ownable detail distinct from any
  generic "nervous tic."
- **Arc**: Not fully drawn at Gate 1 by design (her on-page arc depends
  on Gate 3/4 structural decisions about when and how she appears) — but
  locked as: a person who must be found *and reckoned with*, not
  rescued-and-resolved. Her presence in the book's second half should
  cost the reader some of their sympathy and then re-earn it, not simply
  confirm what they already assumed.
- **Scene utility / indispensability**: The only character who embodies
  "identity as performance" as a genuine counter-worldview to Mara's
  "identity as record" — without her, the book's central question has
  only one side.
- **Stereotype risk**: The "missing girl" trope risk — defined entirely
  by absence and being sought, an object rather than an agent. Guarded
  explicitly, per the source brief, by requiring real flaws, real
  agency, and real culpability on the page once she appears — not
  softened into a symbol because she was gone for most of the book.
- **Research needs**: Light — conservatory/actor-training culture for
  authenticity in her backstory texture; DV-relocation practice
  research (shared with `evelyn_price.md`) informs how her "Lena Hart"
  performance would need to be constructed to be convincing.
