# Investigative Counterpart — Det. Teo Marchetti

STATUS: Gate 2 COMPLETE, AMENDED post-red-team (see
`00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md` amendments 6–7 and
`00_project/red_team_review.md`). Originally invented in the foundation
session (the source brief required "one credible external counterpart"
without naming one); this session ran the required Decision 5
comparison and filled all remaining template fields. This amendment
fixes three findings: an internal contradiction between "bends
procedure exactly once" and a sustained pattern of off-the-record help;
an unresolved professional outcome for the risk his file kept asserting;
and a role conflation between a territorial precinct detective and
NYPD's actual centralized Missing Persons Squad.

## Romance default — Decision 5, CONFIRMED this session

**No conventional romance.** This session's brief required treating the
Gate-1-era default as a live decision, not a self-evidently settled one,
since this character was invented rather than specified — the following
comparison was run before re-confirming it.

**Versions generated and compared:**

1. **No romance, platonic professional ally throughout (SELECTED,
   confirming the prior default).**
2. **Slow-burn mutual attraction, acknowledged but never acted on within
   the book.** Rejected: even unconsummated, sustained romantic tension
   requires real page-time investment across 46 chapters to land
   honestly, and that investment competes directly with
   `00_project/risk_register.md` R-010's standing top priority (the
   sisters' relationship as the book's non-negotiable emotional center).
   It also risks the specific reader letdown of built tension that never
   fires — a structural cost for a benefit (added texture) available more
   cheaply other ways.
3. **A pre-existing romantic history or near-miss predating the book.**
   Rejected: nothing already built requires it, it would need to be
   invented from nothing rather than growing out of established
   material, and it risks reading as shoehorned in. **Partially adopted
   in non-romantic form**: see the new "Prior overlap with Adrienne
   Shaw" section below, which resolves a real open item
   (`relationship_matrix.md`'s cross-check) without romance.
4. **An active, developing romantic subplot that resolves within the
   book.** Rejected on the same grounds as `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`'s
   standing ban on the "love-interest-as-reward" structural beat, and
   because it strains tonal credibility against the story's actual
   timeframe — Elena is missing for five to six weeks
   (`00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md`, Decision 3); a developing
   romance on that timeline risks reading as dramatically frivolous
   against the stakes, not as earned character development.

**Selected: Version 1, confirmed.** Reasoning beyond the original
default: Teo and Mara's dynamic is already thematically complete without
romance — his false belief ("procedure and truth are functionally the
same thing") is a precise, load-bearing mirror of Mara's own
("verification as safety, ambiguity as danger"), and the two of them
each learning to bend their respective system's certainty once, when it
actually matters, is a full, satisfying arc on its own terms. Adding
romantic tension would compete with this clean thematic mirroring rather
than reinforce it — a specific, non-generic reason to keep the default,
not just inertia. Revisit only if Gate 4 finds a specific, non-generic
reason a romantic thread would serve the book better than this
reasoning suggests it would.

## Prior overlap with Adrienne Shaw — resolves `relationship_matrix.md`'s open cross-check

**Locked**: Teo and Adrienne Shaw have had exactly one prior professional
interaction, years before the book begins — an unremarkable case where
an NYPD missing-persons matter briefly intersected with a Meridian
Assurance Group SIU claim (a policyholder who turned out to be the
missing person, alive, uninvolved in anything criminal). No personal
history, no ongoing relationship. Its narrative use: once Adrienne makes
her Part II move against Mara, Teo has an independent, lightly-informed
professional read on her — enough to react with something more specific
than a stranger's neutrality (surprise, or a flicker of "that tracks,"
depending on how Gate 4 wants to play the beat), without the two of them
having any history substantial enough to complicate his primary
relationship with Mara.

## Why this character exists

Per `00_project/gate_1_concept_validation.md`, "why can authorities not
solve it immediately" is the project's single highest plausibility risk.
An investigative counterpart who is an actual NYPD detective, in an
ongoing working (if frequently adversarial) relationship with Mara, is
the mechanism that makes this risk into a dramatized feature rather than
an unaddressed hole — every scene between them is an opportunity to show
real procedural stakes and real jurisdictional limits, rather than
requiring readers to simply accept Mara's unofficial authority.

- **Full name**: Mateo "Teo" Marchetti.
- **Age**: 44.
- **Role**: External investigative counterpart — NYPD Detective,
  Missing Persons Squad (a centralized, citywide specialized unit, not a
  precinct-level assignment — corrected post-red-team; his territorial
  familiarity with the Bellwether House neighborhood, below, comes from
  personal history and having worked that specific area's missing-
  persons cases often over his career, not from being precinct-attached
  to it).
- **Occupation**: NYPD Detective, Missing Persons Squad, working out of
  a citywide unit but with a real, personal, non-administrative tie to
  the Bellwether House neighborhood (see Family and cultural background,
  below).
- **Physical presence**: Workmanlike, unglamorous, visibly overworked —
  written deliberately against the stylized TV-detective archetype; a
  guy with too many open cases and a coffee habit, not a brooding
  antihero.
- **Family and cultural background**: Italian-American, third-generation,
  grew up in Bay Ridge, Brooklyn (walking distance of the kind of
  building stock Bellwether House itself represents — a deliberate,
  small, unstated irony: he knows this neighborhood type intimately,
  which is part of why he's territorial about a civilian working a case
  in it, and part of why this specific case landed with him rather than
  being an arbitrary Missing Persons Squad assignment — he has a
  personal, informal reputation for knowing this part of Brooklyn well). Large extended family, most still local; his own nuclear
  family (ex-wife, one son, age 9) is the one part of his life he keeps
  firmly separate from the extended-family noise. Divorced three years
  before the book begins; sees his son on a fixed, court-ordered
  custody schedule (alternating weekends, one weekday dinner) — a real,
  specific personal stake and source of texture that has nothing to do
  with Mara, deliberately, to avoid the relationship reading as
  romantically coded by default.
- **Education**: Came up through the ranks rather than a criminal-justice
  degree path — an associate's degree from a community college, taken
  slowly, part-time, while already on the job. Consistent with his
  general characterization (workmanlike, unglamorous, not academically
  credentialed the way Mara is) and a small, real class-texture contrast
  with her that neither of them makes a point of.
- **Economic situation**: Middle-class civil servant, stretched by
  child support and NYC cost of living — ordinary, specific, not
  romanticized.
- **Living conditions**: Modest apartment; not a significant location in
  the book unless Gate 4 finds a reason.
- **Habits**: Triages an overloaded caseload constantly; dry,
  pressure-release humor rather than warmth as his default register.
- **Speech pattern**: Economical, procedural, precise about what he can
  and can't say or do given process — this precision is itself a source
  of friction with Mara, who wants faster answers than procedure allows.
- **Private vocabulary**: Real NYPD/missing-persons procedural
  terminology. Research status: `09_research/SOURCE_LOG.md` Entries 1
  and 3 confirm there is no mandatory intake waiting period (do not use
  the "44-hour wait" myth, per `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`) and
  confirm the general shape of civilian/police information boundaries;
  detective-level caseload statistics remain a lower-priority open item
  in `FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md`.
- **Contradictions**: Genuinely cares about his cases despite real
  institutional pressure to close them efficiently; openly skeptical of
  a civilian (however expert) working her own sister's case, while
  grudgingly, and increasingly, recognizing that her expertise is
  finding things his process alone wouldn't.
- **Desire**: Solve his caseload properly, without shortcuts, without
  his own career taking the hit for someone else's improvisation.
- **Need**: To trust an outside expert for once, against every
  institutional instinct that's kept him functional for twenty years.
- **Fear**: A case collapsing — legally or literally — because
  jurisdiction got messy, with real professional consequences for him
  specifically.
- **Shame**: LOCKED, Gate 3: three years ago, a domestic-violence-linked
  missing-persons case where he followed intake procedure exactly —
  correctly, by every standard he was trained to — and the woman was
  found dead before her case was reprioritized as urgent. Nothing he did
  was wrong by the book; the book was wrong, or at least insufficient,
  and he has never fully resolved that. This is the specific, lived
  reason he's capable of eventually bending procedure for Mara when it
  actually matters, rather than being a pure rule-follower throughout —
  not abstract growth, a scar with a name and a case number he still
  remembers.
- **Secret**: Plausibly sympathetic to Mara's read on the case earlier
  than he's officially willing to admit, for procedural/political
  reasons.
- **False belief**: That procedure and truth are functionally the same
  thing — a direct, useful mirror to Mara's own false belief
  (`03_characters/mara_voss.md`), giving the two of them a real,
  specific basis for conflict and eventual mutual respect: two
  systems-literate people who each need to learn a version of the same
  lesson.
- **Relationship to Mara**: Professional friction → grudging respect →
  genuine, platonic working alliance, potentially the ally who helps
  thread the selective-exposure ending (`04_plot/ending_options.md`
  open item), pending Gate 4.
- **Relationship to Elena**: None direct — professional interest only,
  as the subject of his case.
- **Beginning knowledge**: Standard case-file knowledge; skeptical of
  Mara's identity-fraud-pattern theories initially.
- **Ending knowledge**: LOCKED, per `00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md`
  amendments 3–7 — comes to understand Palinode's real scope; is present
  for the climax in a specifically legally-bounded way: he escorts and
  resources the actual rescue as a proper police operation (Mara
  provides intelligence and identification, not tactical participation),
  and is present for the Julian Vale confrontation in a
  consulting/witnessing capacity, with the statement's admissibility
  risk explicitly acknowledged on the page rather than assumed clean.
  **AMENDED, procedure-bending reconciled**: throughout the
  investigation he extends an experienced detective's ordinary,
  individually-defensible discretion toward a credentialed professional
  he's coming to trust — confirming things that are technically
  discoverable anyway, meeting to compare notes, general procedural
  guidance — none of which is the real risk. **The one genuine
  jeopardy** is a single, specific act at the point Adrienne Shaw has
  already cut Mara off institutionally: sharing a specific piece of
  protected case information he has no defensible cover for, a direct,
  personal echo of his own three-years-ago case. This is what actually
  risks his career, distinct from the lower-stakes discretion around it
  — the two are no longer described in contradictory terms.
- **Professional outcome, NEW, LOCKED at the same depth as Adrienne
  Shaw's ending**: he faces an Internal Affairs inquiry over the
  disclosure and his role in the case's resolution. He survives it, but
  not unscathed — a formal reprimand in his file, and passed over for a
  promotion he'd been positioning for. The department's real
  institutional incentive not to make an example of the detective who
  helped break a decades-old homicide and a major fraud network is what
  keeps this survivable; it is a real, named, permanent cost, not a
  private satisfaction with no consequence.
- **Behavioral tells**: Taps a closed fist against his sternum, once,
  when he's about to say something he's not sure is his place to say —
  a small, specific, physical tell distinct from any other character's.
  **AMENDED post-red-team** (the original second tell, "goes quiet and
  unusually still," collided with Evelyn Price's tell mechanism):
  flips open and checks his own notebook or phone for a specific old
  case number he already has memorized, an unnecessary physical action
  that buys him a beat, when a case reminds him of the one he got
  exactly right by the book and still lost.
- **Arc**: Procedural skeptic → extends ordinary, defensible professional
  discretion increasingly → one genuine, jeopardizing act of real
  procedure-bending at the moment it actually matters → survives an IAB
  inquiry with real, lasting professional cost. Not a character who
  abandons professionalism, one who exercises judgment within it and
  pays a real price for the one time he goes further than judgment
  alone would justify.
- **Scene utility / indispensability**: The dramatized answer to
  `risk_register.md` R-006 — without him, Mara's lack of official
  authority is an unaddressed plausibility hole; with him, it's a
  recurring, textured source of real conflict and eventual alliance.
- **Stereotype risk**: "Gruff but good cop" is a genre stock type;
  guarded by his specific personal texture (the custody schedule, the
  particular past case, dry rather than brooding humor) rather than
  generic gruffness standing in for characterization.
- **Research needs**: Substantially resolved — see
  `09_research/SOURCE_LOG.md` Entries 3 and 4. Remaining open item:
  real detective caseload/case-prioritization statistics, logged at
  medium priority in `FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md`, useful for texture but not
  blocking.
