# Mara Voss

STATUS: Gate 2 COMPLETE. Stereotype-risk and consistency review recorded
in `00_project/gate_2_character_validation.md`.

- **Full name**: Mara Voss, born **Mara Marsh**. "Voss" is the surname
  Palinode assigned the family when Mara was 13, following her father
  Peter Marsh's murder — see
  `00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md` Decision 2 for the full,
  locked origin. Mara does not know this at the start of the book (see
  Beginning knowledge, below); "Marsh" is never spoken in her presence
  in Part I or early Part II by anyone still using it, since her mother
  Anna and Elena both know better.
- **Date of birth / precise age marker**: 36 at story present; was 13
  at the time of the family's relocation, 23 years before story present
  — both figures cross-checked and locked in
  `gate_3_mystery_validation.md`.
- **Age**: 36.
- **Role**: Primary protagonist, dominant POV (~85–95% of the book).
- **Occupation**: Senior investigator, Special Investigations Unit,
  Meridian Assurance Group (fictional insurer, invented this session —
  name may be revised at Gate 2 if research suggests a more plausible
  fictional-insurer naming convention). Specializes in complex identity
  fraud: staged deaths, fabricated claims, manipulated records, false
  relationships, synthetic identities, invented personal histories.
- **Physical presence**: Trained stillness — the specific physical
  control of someone who has spent a career in interview rooms where
  fidgeting reads as guilt. Dresses to be forgettable in the field
  (unremarkable, well-made, non-memorable clothing, chosen deliberately,
  not out of indifference) and slightly sharper in the office. Not
  described through a single "tell" scar or old-injury device (avoiding
  a specific device flagged as reuse-adjacent in
  `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`) — her physicality is legible
  through *behavior* (how still she holds a room) more than through a
  marked feature.
- **Family and cultural background**: Manufactured. The "Voss" family
  background is deliberately unremarkable and thin — presented,
  consistent with `02_novel_bible/palinode_system.md`'s realism rule
  that easy-to-manufacture identities have weak, hard-to-verify-further
  ties, as a generic Midwestern-American family of vaguely
  German-surname extraction, with no strong extended-family network to
  slip up in front of. Her actual birth family's background and
  ethnicity are unknown to her and to this bible — intentionally
  withheld, a Gate 3 decision, not an oversight.
- **Education**: A large public state university (unnamed — avoid a real
  named institution), business/finance-adjacent degree, no
  particularly privileged pedigree — consistent with the manufactured
  family's deliberately unremarkable economic profile.
- **Economic situation**: Solidly self-made middle-to-upper-middle
  class via her career, not inherited money. Financially self-
  sufficient to a degree that reads as slightly compulsive — a
  plausible, specific echo of growing up in a family whose financial
  footing was, unbeknownst to her for most of her life, itself
  constructed.
- **Living conditions**: Her own Brooklyn or lower-Manhattan apartment
  pre-investigation; competent, guarded, under-decorated in a way that
  reads as "doesn't expect to be seen here by people who matter" —
  full build-out deferred to Gate 4 once the plot requires specific
  scenes there.
- **Habits**: Early riser. Keeps meticulous personal records (calendars,
  receipts, correspondence) with a thoroughness that mirrors her
  professional method — a specific, revealing detail: she applies her
  investigative rigor to her *own* life constantly, except to the one
  relationship that most needed it.
- **Speech pattern**: Precise, spare, asks more questions than she
  volunteers answers. Uses professional vocabulary as an emotional
  shield in personal conversations — a fight with Elena or a hard
  conversation with Adrienne Shaw will surface phrases like "material
  misrepresentation" or "pattern of loss" in contexts where they're
  slightly, tellingly wrong.
- **Private vocabulary**: SIU/fraud-investigation jargon — claimant,
  material misrepresentation, pattern of loss, documented inconsistency.
  **Amended post-red-team**: "red-flag indicator" removed from this
  list — it sits too close to `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`'s ban
  on a character's internal noticing-vocabulary being verb-anchored on
  "flagging." If a term for this concept is needed in dialogue or a
  document quotation, use "documented inconsistency" or "material
  misrepresentation" instead; never let "flag/flagging" become her
  internal narration verb for noticing something. *Research flag*:
  verify this vocabulary against real insurance-SIU practice before
  heavy use in dialogue —
  queued in `09_research/FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md`.
- **Contradictions**: The most disciplined investigator in her
  professional life is the person who let exactly one relationship go
  uninvestigated for years — she applies total rigor everywhere except
  the one place it might have cost her the most to apply it, and knows,
  on some level, that this is not an accident.
- **Desire (surface want)**: Find Elena. Understand the seven
  identities.
- **Need (what must change for real growth)**: To act on trust once,
  deliberately, without requiring proof first — and to survive doing so.
- **Fear**: That Elena was telling the truth in the voicemail and Mara
  chose not to believe her — and, underneath that, a newer fear once the
  investigation starts: that verifying her own origin will cost her the
  version of herself she's spent her adult life building.
- **Shame**: That her first instinct, even in the moment she learned
  Elena might be in real danger, was self-protective precision — editing
  her own statement — rather than an unguarded response.
- **Secret**: Altered one detail in the statement she gave police about
  Elena's final voicemail, because the complete message makes Mara
  appear culpable for how she responded to it.
- **False belief**: "If I can prove what happened, I will know what is
  true." (Conflates factual, legal, emotional, and moral truth as if
  they were one axis — the book's central thematic error, embodied in
  one person; see `02_novel_bible/thematic_argument.md`.)
- **Relationship to Elena**: Younger sister. Loved badly, in the book's
  own words — Mara demands verifiable proof, Elena experiences that
  demand as a refusal of love. Full dynamic in
  `02_novel_bible/master_premise.md` and `relationship_matrix.md`.
- **Beginning knowledge**: Does not know Elena is entangled with
  Palinode or in danger; does not know her own family identity is
  manufactured; believes her own childhood record is straightforwardly
  true.
- **Ending knowledge**: The truth of Palinode, her own manufactured
  origin (her birth name, her father Peter Marsh's real death, her
  mother's decision), and, per the locked ending
  (`04_plot/final_ending.md`), the full mechanism of Elena's
  disappearance and enough of what to do with all of it to make a real,
  costly choice at the climax.
- **A childhood memory, now specified**: Mara has always carried one
  hazy, decontextualized memory of a walk-up apartment building she's
  never been able to place — a smell, a particular creak of stairs, a
  woman's voice she associates with safety rather than fear. She has
  never treated this as significant. The investigation recontextualizes
  it with real force once she understands what it actually was. This is
  a character/emotional beat for Gate 4 to place, not a formal
  fair-play clue in `05_mystery/clue_register.md` — it belongs to Mara
  alone until she chooses to voice it, if she does.
- **Lie (ongoing behavior, distinct from the specific secret above)**:
  Maintains, to herself and others, that the estrangement from Elena
  doesn't especially affect her and that she's pursuing the case
  primarily out of professional obligation/competence rather than
  devastation.
- **Reason for the lie**: Professional identity is her armor; admitting
  how much Elena matters risks a version of herself she doesn't trust
  herself to function as.
- **Behavioral tells**: Over-explains procedural detail when emotionally
  cornered. Answers an emotional question with a clarifying question.
  Reaches for a phone, file, or document when anxious, the way another
  person might reach for a person.
- **Arc**: Certainty → destabilization → self-suspicion → moral agency.
- **Scene utility / indispensability**: The only character whose
  professional lens makes the seven-witness pattern legible as a system
  rather than noise, and the only character for whom the investigation
  is simultaneously an external mystery and a direct reopening of her
  own defining relational wound. No other character could do both jobs
  at once.
- **Stereotype risk**: The controlled, unemotional, high-competence woman
  protagonist risks an "ice queen learns to feel" arc if handled
  carelessly. Explicit guard rail: her growth is specifically about
  *trust preceding proof*, a precise, one-axis change — not a general
  softening, not resolved by a romance (see
  `investigative_counterpart.md`'s no-conventional-romance default), and
  she should remain recognizably herself (precise, controlled, dry) at
  the end, not "warmed" into a different personality.
- **Research needs**: Substantially resolved — see
  `09_research/SOURCE_LOG.md` Entries 3 and 4 (SIU statutory authority is
  real and bounded; civilian/police information boundaries confirmed).
  Directly grounds `risk_register.md` R-006; see
