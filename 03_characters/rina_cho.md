- **Age**: 34.
- **Role**: Witness — knew the missing woman as a documentary subject/
  research contact, researching a film about people erased from public
  records (a deliberate thematic echo — a documentary *about* identity
  erasure, discovered by a character investigating a real instance of
  it).
- **Occupation**: Documentary editor / archival producer, freelance.
- **Physical presence**: Works odd, irregular hours; an editor's
  trained eye for visual detail and sequence, which she can't quite turn
  off in ordinary conversation.
- **Family and cultural background**: Korean-American; specific family
  and generational detail (immigrant-generation status, family
  relationships) to be built with real specificity at Gate 2 rather than

  institution).
- **Economic situation**: Freelance, project-to-project, genuinely
  precarious — a deliberate parallel to Elena's own actor-economy

  relationship in-story is professional/research-based.
- **Living conditions**: Apartment doubles as a small edit bay — hard
  drives, monitors, archive boxes.
- **Habits**: Professionally trained to notice how footage and narrative
  can be constructed to seem true (framing, sequence, selective cuts) —
  a genuinely useful meta-skill for the mystery, since she understands
  manufactured narrative from the inside without yet realizing she's
  been inside one personally.
- **Speech pattern**: Precise about sequence and framing; thinks and
  talks in something close to "frame-level" terms even about her own
  memories.
- **Private vocabulary**: Editing/production jargon (cut, coverage,
  release, footage log).
- **Contradictions**: Professionally expert at spotting manufactured
  narrative in other people's material; didn't spot it in her own
  research relationship, because the story was, by her own professional
  standards, too well-made to doubt.
- **Desire**: Finish and protect her documentary project; understand
  what actually happened to her research subject/contact.
- **Need**: To apply her professional skepticism to her own experience,
  not only to the footage she edits for other people.
- **Fear**: Her raw footage being lost, corrupted, or — worse — turning
  out to be evidence of something genuinely dangerous she didn't sign up
  to be involved in.
- **Shame**: Didn't push harder on inconsistencies because the material
  was compelling and she wanted it to be real/usable.
- **Secret**: Holds specific raw footage more revealing than she's
  realized — she's been looking at it as a filmmaker, not as evidence.
- **False belief**: That if something is on camera, consistently, across
  multiple sessions, it's real.
- **Relationship to Mara**: Professional-to-professional mutual respect,
  once established — two people who both work in "is this account
  true" for a living, from different disciplines.
- **Relationship to Elena**: Research-subject/contact relationship,
  genuine mutual professional fascination rather than close personal
  friendship.
- **Beginning knowledge**: Has raw footage and research notes she
  hasn't fully re-examined since the disappearance.
- **Ending knowledge**: Realizes the actual evidentiary weight of what
  she's been sitting on.
- **Behavioral tells**: TBD, Gate 2.
- **Arc**: Has evidence without recognizing its full value → applies her
  professional skepticism to her own life for the first time.
- **Scene utility / indispensability**: Provides literal physical/
  recorded evidence as clue material, and carries the "editing
  manufactures apparent truth" theme directly and specifically (distinct
  from the paper/testimony evidence every other witness provides).
- **Stereotype risk**: Avoid "quirky artsy millennial" shorthand — her
