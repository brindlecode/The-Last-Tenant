# Master Synopsis

## Setup

Mara Voss, a senior insurance-fraud investigator who has built her life
around verification, has spent years managing her relationship with her
unpredictable younger sister Elena by keeping her at arm's length —
demanding proof before belief, treating Elena's instability as a known
quantity rather than a warning. When Elena leaves a fragmented, urgent
voicemail, Mara does what she's always done: she sets it aside (Opening,
Option 2 — see `opening_options.md`).

## Inciting incident

Elena is confirmed missing. Her last known address is Apartment 4C,
Bellwether House, a Brooklyn walk-up — a unit management records
insist has been legally vacant for twelve years.

## Rising investigation (Part I, roughly Ch. 1–12)

Mara begins working the case both informally alongside NYPD Detective
Teo Marchetti (real jurisdictional friction, not easy cooperation — see
`03_characters/investigative_counterpart.md`) and through her own
professional access to records. She starts interviewing residents and
finds seven separate, sincere accounts of a woman matching Elena's
description, living under seven different names, professions, and
personal histories — Evelyn Price's "Lena Hart" (a pregnant woman
fleeing an abusive partner), Jonah Bell's "Dana Kessler" (a legal
researcher helping clients disappear from coercive marriages), Irena
Petrov's much older memory of "Anna," Malik Reed's glimpses of a woman
using the building's disused service routes, Rina Cho's documentary
research contact, and Calvin Moss's remote records-work colleague.
Gabriel Knox, the superintendent, is evasive. Part I builds toward its
climax: Mara, during an authorized apartment inspection, finds a
concealed cache of identity documents — all bearing Elena's face, none
sharing a name — and an envelope addressed specifically to her. Inside:
"You were the first tenant." (Relocated here from the original opening
option — see `opening_options.md`'s Part-I-climax recommendation.)

## Deepening mystery (Part II, roughly Ch. 13–34)

Mara's investigation surfaces the Palinode Continuity Group, a private
identity-engineering network founded by Julian Vale, and she begins to
understand that the "vacancy" of Apartment 4C is a deliberate paperwork
fiction (`02_novel_bible/bellwether_house.md`) covering decades of
manufactured tenancy. Her own inquiry starts to reveal that her family's
own history is not what she believed — Irena Petrov's memory of "Anna"
likely describes Mara and Elena's own mother, processed through this
same building, decades earlier, fleeing a danger connected to the girls'
father. Adrienne Shaw, Mara's mentor and supervisor, is revealed to have
suppressed Palinode-linked claim patterns for years, complicating Mara's
professional footing. Julian Vale makes contact, directly or indirectly,
and Mara learns he regards her as living proof his work was justified —
Palinode's first, and by his account most successful, "continuity" case.
Part II ends on a major betrayal/reversal (exact mechanism TBD, Gate 3)
that removes any remaining safety in Mara's position.

## Climax and resolution (Part III, roughly Ch. 35–46)

Mara locates Elena — alive, but deeply compromised by her own
entanglement with Palinode and unable to safely resume her original
identity. The climax (selected Ending Architecture 1, refined with
Architecture 3's trust-based beat — see `ending_options.md`) turns on
Mara choosing selective exposure: bringing down Julian Vale, Palinode's
criminal clients, and its financial infrastructure, while protecting the
legitimately sheltered identities Palinode also built — a decision made,
at its most critical point, by choosing to act on Elena's judgment
rather than her own fully verified evidence. Mara ends the book having
used identity-construction principles herself, deliberately and
transparently, to help build Elena a new, sustainable life — an ironic
completion of her arc, chosen rather than imposed. The sisters reach a
real, if not uncomplicated, reconciliation.
