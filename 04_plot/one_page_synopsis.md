# One-Page Synopsis

Mara Voss investigates identity fraud for a living — staged deaths,
manufactured claims, synthetic lives — and has spent years applying the
same instinct to her own family, demanding proof from her unpredictable
younger sister Elena before she'll offer belief. When Elena vanishes
after a fragmented, urgent voicemail Mara initially dismisses, Mara
finally has to reckon with what that instinct has cost.

Elena's last known address is Apartment 4C, Bellwether House, a Brooklyn
walk-up that management records insist has sat legally vacant for
twelve years. Seven residents remember otherwise — each recalling a
woman matching Elena's description under a different name, profession,
and personal history, every account carrying real, uncontradicted
evidence. No one is lying. They still can't all be describing the same
ordinary life.

The investigation surfaces the Palinode Continuity Group, a private
network that has spent decades manufacturing not just false documents
but false *continuity* — real neighbors, real memories, real
relationships built around invented people. And it surfaces something
Mara didn't expect to find: proof that her own family's history was
itself manufactured, that Elena went to Bellwether House not primarily
to hide, but to prove it — and that Mara's entire career, spent
detecting exactly this kind of engineered life, exists because she grew
up inside one.

As Mara gets closer to Elena and to the truth, she has to work around,
alongside, and against the limits of actual police jurisdiction, the
compromised silence of her own mentor, and Palinode's founder, Julian
Vale — a man who regards Mara herself as his organization's greatest
success. When she finds Elena, damaged and unable to safely resume her
old life, Mara faces the book's real question in its sharpest form:
whether the truth she's spent her whole life demanding is worth more
than the people it would cost to expose completely — and whether she can,
for the first time, act on someone else's judgment instead of her own
verified proof.

She chooses selective exposure over total exposure: Palinode's
leadership and crimes brought down, its legitimately protected
identities left standing, and Elena given — this time by choice, and by
someone who loves her — the kind of new, honest beginning Palinode was
never able to offer anyone.
