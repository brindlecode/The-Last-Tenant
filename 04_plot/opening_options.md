# Opening Options

STATUS: Gate 1 deliverable, COMPLETE. Selection is a working choice,
revisable at Gate 4 once full structure exists. Per
`00_project/originality_policy.md`, treatment descriptions below are
synopsis-level (beats, function, rationale) — no drafted prose. Actual
chapter prose is out of scope for this repository until Gate 5 closes.

Five structurally distinct openings were generated and compared, per
`00_project/quality_gates.md` Gate 1's requirement. None is an
"unidentified woman in danger," a generic murder scene, a dream, a
future flash, or a misleading POV gimmick — all five were checked
against that exclusion list specifically.

---

## Option 1 — "The Hidden Cache" (source-brief default; physical staging
revised post-red-team, see below)

**Beats**: Mara, granted brief authorized access to Apartment 4C tied to
a genuine Meridian claim (`00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md`
amendment 1), finds the apartment deliberately, professionally emptied.
Her eye for records and structure both catches that the unit's sealed,
disused dumbwaiter shaft has been recently disturbed; she finds Elena's
hidden evidence cache inside it — identity documents, several, all
bearing Elena's face, none of them Elena's name, plus Elena's own
investigative notes and partial financial records. Among them, an
envelope addressed specifically to Mara. Inside: "You were the first
tenant."

**Physical staging note, AMENDED post-red-team**: originally specified
as "a concealed cavity" behind "an inconsistency in a wall or fixture."
Two independent review perspectives found this sat too close to
`01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`'s explicit ban on "a single hidden
physical cavity/patch in a wall functioning as the central physical-
evidence mechanism the whole mystery pivots on." **Relocated to the
building's sealed, disused dumbwaiter shaft** — already-established
architecture (`02_novel_bible/bellwether_house.md`), structurally and
imagistically distinct from a wall cavity, and with real thematic
resonance a plain wall patch lacked (an old vertical channel that once
carried messages between floors, repurposed one last time to carry
Elena's message to Mara). Full reasoning:
`00_project/gate_3_mystery_validation.md` amendment 8.

**Function if used as the opening**: immediate, maximal mystery hook;
personal stakes established in the first scene.

**Risk**: This line is written, in the source brief, as something Mara
"eventually discovers" — it's pitched as a mid/late-book gut-punch, not
day-one information. Opening literally on it front-loads the single
biggest revelation in the entire novel before the reader has any
relationship with Mara or Elena to feel its weight against. It also
requires Mara to have inspection access to a missing person's apartment
essentially immediately, which is a small but real plausibility cost
paid for a payoff the book needs much later.

**Verdict**: Too valuable to spend here. Recommended for relocation to
the Part I climax (~end of Chapter 12) instead — see
`00_project/gate_1_concept_validation.md` Part B4 and (once built)
`04_plot/chapter_map.md`.

## Option 2 — "The Voicemail" (SELECTED)

**Beats**: Open on Mara in an ordinary moment of competence — mid-
interview or mid-document-review on an unrelated case, established
efficient and precise — when Elena's voicemail notification surfaces
(already old; this isn't the first time Mara has seen it). She
half-listens, recognizes the tone as "an Elena thing," and consciously
sets it aside to finish what she's doing, the way she's done before. The
chapter's second half escalates: a call (landlord, building management,
or a friend of Elena's) that Elena hasn't been seen in longer than fits
the pattern. Mara, alone that night, finally plays the full voicemail —
fragmented, urgent in a way her prior dismissals didn't register — and
the chapter ends there, on what she now can't undismiss.

**Function**: puts the book's actual subject — Mara choosing
verification/routine over her sister, in real time, on the page, not as
backstory — before the plot mechanism. Satisfies "immediate disturbance
in the opening pages" through the discomfort of watching the dismissal
happen, not through incident. Naturally reaches the inciting incident
(confirmation Elena is missing) within the same or following chapter,
comfortably inside the required ~8–10% window.

**Risk**: Less conventionally "hooky" in a plot-mechanical sense than
Options 1 or 3; some readers/gatekeepers expect faster plot ignition.
Mitigated by making the dismissal itself viscerally uncomfortable to
read — tension from character, not incident, but real tension.

**Why selected over the alternatives**: it is the only option that puts
the book's stated non-negotiable priority (the sisters' relationship as
emotional core, never overwhelmed by mystery machinery) at the very
front, structurally, rather than trusting later chapters to establish it
after the plot has already claimed the reader's primary attention.

## Option 3 — "In Medias Res, Resident Interview"

**Beats**: Open already inside Bellwether House, mid-interview with one
resident (working choice: Evelyn Price) describing "Lena Hart" in warm,
specific, confident detail. The reader (and Mara) simply watch a
competent interview happen — until one detail doesn't scan (a date, a
description) and the first crack shows. Backstory (why Mara is there,
the disappearance, the voicemail) is folded in afterward, once the
scene's hook is already set.

**Function**: maximum immediate immersion in the book's single most
distinctive mechanism — an account of "Elena" that is entirely genuine
and entirely wrong. Shows the premise instead of explaining it.

**Risk**: Real disorientation risk — opening with no grounding in who
Mara is or why she's there asks a lot of a first page. Must be executed
so it reads as Mara-not-yet-fully-oriented-either (true at this early
point) rather than as the narration withholding orientation to seem
clever, which the prose rules explicitly ban
(`01_author_bible/prose_style_guide.md`).

**Verdict**: Strong second choice. Flagged as the fallback if Gate 4
structural work concludes Option 2 builds too slowly for the intended
pace.

## Option 4 — "The Vacancy Record" (cold document open)

**Beats**: A few lines of dry institutional language — a property or
management record stating Apartment 4C vacant, twelve years — precede
the first scene with Mara, establishing the book's central irony
(official truth vs. lived truth) before any character appears.

**Function**: extremely efficient; states the premise's core paradox in
the fewest possible words.

**Risk**: "Cold document flash, then cut to character" is a common
enough thriller device to risk reading as furniture rather than a fresh
choice. Also delays the reader's connection to Mara as a person, working
against the emotional-core priority.

**Verdict**: Rejected as a full opening architecture. Recommended instead
as a possible short epigraph-style fragment ahead of Option 2's scene (a
few lines, not a scene of its own) if Gate 4 wants the paradox stated
even more explicitly up front — a device decision, not a structural one,
deferred to that gate.

---

## Option 5 — "The Argument" (dramatized last-contact scene)

**Beats**: Open on the sisters' actual last real conversation before the
disappearance — dramatized in full scene, not summarized — showing
Mara's demand for something verifiable and Elena's experience of that
demand as rejection. Cut forward (clearly, chronologically, no gimmick)
to the point the book's present-day action begins.

**Function**: the most direct possible dramatization of the book's
thematic argument; lets the reader witness, rather than be told about,
the dynamic Mara will spend the book reckoning with.

**Risk**: Structurally resembles the "flash to before, then jump
forward" shape the brief explicitly warns against, even though it isn't
a manipulative flash-forward gimmick in the banned sense (no
unidentified-danger cold open, no misleading POV trick) — it still
risks *reading* like the pattern being excluded. High execution risk for
a marginal gain over Option 2, which achieves a similar effect (showing,
not telling, the sisters' dynamic) with less structural risk.

**Verdict**: Rejected as the opening. Preserved as strong material for an
early flashback/memory scene later in Part I or Part II, properly framed
as Mara's own remembering rather than a structural device — not wasted,
relocated.
