## 1. The twelve-year vacancy record

- **First appearance**: Part I.
- **Surface meaning**: Apartment 4C has been legally uninhabited for
  twelve years — official confirmation nothing is happening there.
- **True meaning**: Marks only the lapse of a *separate*, unconnected
  synthetic-tenant lease (`palinode_timeline.md`) — not the family's own
  much older, off-record history there, and not evidence the unit is
  actually empty now.
- **Misread by**: Mara, initially, and the reader — the "twelve years"
  figure is a real record, honestly reported by management, which makes
  it a fair red herring rather than a lie: it's true and irrelevant to
  present-day occupancy, a distinction the story earns by making Mara's
  own professional skepticism the thing that eventually questions it.
- **Payoff**: Part II, once the shell-entity/lapsed-lease mechanism is
  fully traced.
- **Fairness check**: PASS — the record itself never changes or is shown
  to be forged; only its interpretation does, consistent with
  `genre_contract.md`'s "genuine and partial, not a lie" rule.

## 2. The inconsistent apartment-numbering scheme

- **First appearance**: Part I, background texture; foregrounded once a
  piece of evidence is dated to the old scheme.
- **Surface meaning**: Building quirk/old-building disorganization.
- **True meaning**: Explains why some older evidence (Irena's account,
  older building records) appears to contradict current unit
  identification — not a contradiction once the renumbering history is
  understood.
- **Misread by**: Mara, briefly, as a potential inconsistency undermining
  a witness's reliability (most likely to threaten Irena Petrov's
  credibility if mishandled — precisely the reading her stereotype-risk
  guard requires the narrative not to endorse).
- **Payoff**: Part I–II, ideally resolved by Mara's own records
  diligence, giving her a small, early, earned "I was wrong to doubt
  that witness" beat that seeds her larger arc.
- **Fairness check**: PASS — a real, physically documented building
  history fact (`bellwether_house.md`), not authorial sleight of hand.

## 3. Gabriel Knox's analog maintenance logs

- **First appearance**: Part I (his evasiveness); logs' actual content
  revealed gradually across Part I–II.
- **Surface meaning**: An old-fashioned, possibly meaningless paper
  habit; his refusal to produce them reads as stonewalling.
- **True meaning**: The single most objectively reliable evidence source
  in the book — real, dated, physically hard to retroactively fake — and
  his withholding is a specific, guilty choice, not disorganization.
- **Misread by**: Mara and the reader may initially suspect Gabriel is a
  more central, active villain than he is (see Red Herring Register #1)
  — fair, because his evasiveness is real and his complicity, while
  smaller in scope than it first appears, is also real.
- **Payoff**: Part II (partial access) through Part III (the specific
  entries proving the final weeks' activity spike, and, once Mara
  understands the mechanism, entries showing his self-protective report
  was a distinct, escalated act beyond his original arrangement).
- **Fairness check**: PASS — his motive (job/home security) is
  established before the reader has reason to doubt it.

## 4. Theater-craft vocabulary in witness accounts

- **First appearance**: Part I–II, scattered across multiple witness
  interviews.
- **Surface meaning**: Incidental color in how different witnesses
  describe "her."
- **True meaning**: A civilian witness wouldn't independently produce a
  trained performer's specific phrasing ("blocking," "a beat," "staying
  in it") — a pattern only Mara's specific professional
  pattern-recognition, or a reader paying close attention across
  multiple interviews, would connect.
- **Misread by**: Nobody actively misreads this — it's a genuine,
  fairly-clued pattern requiring the reader to notice repetition across
  scenes, the purest fair-play clue in the register.
- **Payoff**: Part II, when Mara consciously identifies the pattern as
  evidence of a single trained performer behind all seven accounts.
- **Fairness check**: PASS — the strongest, cleanest clue in the book;
  requires attentive reading, not privileged information.

## 12. Elena's investigative notes — organizational scope, NEW post-red-team

- **First appearance**: Part I climax, inside the relocated cache
  (clue #11) — a distinct clue in its own right, not a sub-detail of
  #11, because it does different fair-play work.
- **Surface meaning**: Elena's own handwritten research notes and
  partial financial records, found alongside the identity documents.
- **True meaning**: This is what gives Mara — and the reader — the fair,
  direct leap from "one family's manufactured identity" (a single
  apartment, a single set of documents) to "a whole organization with a
  name, a founder, and other clients." The notes include Elena's own
  reference to "Palinode" and "the Continuity Group" by name (learned
  during her operative period) and evidence the pattern extends beyond
  the Voss family specifically. **Closes a Critical fairness-audit gap**:
  the prior register had no clue actually delivering organizational
  scope — reveal 5 in `reveal_ladder.md` was undercued. See
  `gate_3_mystery_validation.md` amendment 9 and
  `evidence_provenance.md`.
- **Misread by**: N/A — this is the clue that resolves the earlier,
  narrower misreading (that Elena acted alone, for reasons of her own)
  rather than itself being misread.
- **Payoff**: Part II, once Mara has had time to research and confirm
  what the notes reference — not instantaneous at discovery, which would
  compress reveal 5 into reveal 4 and undercut both.
- **Fairness check**: PASS — the notes are Elena's own genuine work
  product, not an authorial info-dump; their existence is fully
  consistent with her established ~2-year evidence-gathering campaign.

## 14. The Dana Kessler Meridian policy, NEW post-red-team

- **First appearance**: Part I, before the Bellwether House interviews
  begin — this is what sends Mara there with genuine professional
  standing in the first place.
- **Surface meaning**: An obscure, lapsed small life-insurance policy
  flagged by routine SIU compliance screening.
- **True meaning**: The policy is in the name of "Dana Kessler," one of
  Elena's constructed identities — Meridian, Mara's own employer,
  unknowingly insured her sister's fake identity years earlier. This is
  the claim anchor that gives Mara real, bounded professional authority
  to be at Bellwether House at all — closing a Critical finding that the
  original mystery had Mara operating there with no stated authorization
  mechanism. See `gate_3_mystery_validation.md` amendment 1.
- **Misread by**: N/A — this clue's job is to establish standing, not to
  mislead.
- **Payoff**: Its full significance (that it's Elena's own alias) lands
  the moment Mara connects the policyholder to the missing-person case,
  early in Part I — an immediate payoff, unlike this register's other,
  slower-burning clues.
- **Fairness check**: PASS — grounded in real SIU statutory practice
  (`09_research/SOURCE_LOG.md` Entry 4) and gives Mara's presence at
  Bellwether House a genuine professional basis throughout Part I and
  much of Part II, with an explicit, visible boundary (Gate 4 to
  dramatize) where her legitimate claim-authority ends and her personal
  involvement, unofficially, begins.
