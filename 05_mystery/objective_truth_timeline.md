## Chronology

- **~38 years before story present**: Julian Vale, working in
  government-adjacent victim/witness relocation, becomes convinced
  official protection is too narrow. Founds the earliest version of
  Palinode — genuinely altruistic at this stage.
  (`02_novel_bible/palinode_system.md`.)
- **~30 years before story present**: Dr. Priya Ansari, an actuarial
  scientist and credit-systems researcher disillusioned with mainstream
  financial-verification infrastructure, joins as senior partner,
  giving Palinode its records/credit-system sophistication.
  (`03_characters/supporting_cast.md`.)
- **~30–24 years before story present**: Funding pressure begins
  Palinode's shift from purely altruistic cases toward paying clients,
  gradually including clients escaping accountability rather than
  danger. Mission drift, not a single corrupting event.
- **24 years before story present**: Peter Marsh, a title-insurance and
  property-records examiner in an unnamed mid-sized Pennsylvania city,
  discovers a regional property-title and synthetic-identity fraud
  scheme in the course of his ordinary audit work. Reports internally;
  escalates to a state regulatory contact when his employer is
  unresponsive. Is threatened through an intermediary.
- **23 years before story present**: Peter Marsh is killed — a staged
  single-vehicle accident. His widow, Anna Marsh, now solely responsible
  for two daughters (Mara, 13; Elena, 9) and in real danger herself, is
  connected through a contact Peter had trusted to Julian Vale's
  network. **Julian personally handles the case** — it reads to him as
  close to an ideal validation of Palinode's founding purpose. The
  family is processed at **Bellwether House, Apartment 4C**, over a
  period of weeks to a few months while their "Voss" identities are
  built. First names are deliberately preserved (a genuine Palinode
  practice — changing a young child's first name risks behavioral
  slippage in front of casual witnesses); surnames and biographical
  history are replaced. **Irena Petrov, already a Bellwether House
  resident, meets and remembers Anna during this window.** Once
  processing is complete, the family relocates permanently to a modest
  Long Island town — not Bellwether House — where Mara and Elena
  actually grow up as "Voss." Mara and Elena grow up knowing their
  father died in an accident when they were young; this part is true.
  What is withheld is that it wasn't random, that their identity was
  reconstructed in its aftermath, and why.
- **23 years ago to ~5 years before story present**: Mara grows up as
  "Mara Voss" with no reason to question her identity's construction.
  Elena grows up alongside her; over early adulthood, gradually works
  out more of the family's true origin than Mara does (a document
  Elena finds among her mother's things in her early twenties is the
  working mechanism, Gate 4 to dramatize if a scene requires it).
- **~8–6 years before story present**: Elena's theater/conservatory
  training and inconsistent acting work; during a financially lean
  period, she is recruited into paid Palinode-adjacent continuity/
  performance work, initially without understanding the organization's
  full scope.
- **~4–3 years before story present**: Elena, now a skilled operative,
  builds a full continuity identity for a paying client relocating to
  escape a credible abuse allegation tied to his position running a
  private youth program.
- **~2.5–2 years before story present**: Elena learns, through a
  contact in her performance/documentary-adjacent world, that this
  client re-offended under his new identity. **This is Elena's
  whistleblower trigger** — a specific, addressed harm she enabled, not
  abstract guilt. She begins quietly gathering proof against Palinode,
  motivated by leverage, penance, and a wish to stop the organization
  without destroying the people it had also legitimately protected.
- **~2 years before story present**: Elena's own research leads her to
  recognize her family's history from the inside and to identify
  Bellwether House as the site of their original processing. She
  begins constructing the first of what will become seven separate,
  genuine identities among the building's residents, using Palinode's
  own core principle (multiple independent-seeming confirmations)
  against it.
- **~12 years before story present**: The last official paper record
  touching Apartment 4C — a synthetic/deceased-identity lease held via
  a Palinode-linked shell entity, unconnected to the Marsh/Voss
  processing itself, which left no lease record at all — formally
  lapses. No new filing occurs from this point forward. (This is a
  separate mechanism from the Marsh family's own brief, off-record stay
  twenty-three years ago; see `02_novel_bible/bellwether_house.md`.)
  Gabriel Knox is paid, by a Palinode intermediary, to not report
  irregular foot traffic and utility use in the unit — established well
  before Elena's arrival.
- **Roughly 14–18 months before story present, through the
  disappearance**: Elena is functionally present as all seven witness-
  facing identities, in a staggered sequence — Gabriel Knox and Irena
  Petrov (permanent residents) encountered earliest; Evelyn Price and
  Jonah Bell's relationships developing in the middle of the window;
  Rina Cho's documentary-research contact and Calvin Moss's remote
  records work established later, closer to the point her evidence-
  gathering intensifies; Malik Reed's sightings cluster in the final
  two to three months. She hides her accumulating evidence archive
  inside Apartment 4C itself — the safest available location, since the
  unit is officially off-record. She prepares a contingency envelope
  addressed to Mara, containing "You were the first tenant," in case
  she is stopped before she can deliver the proof in person.
- **Final weeks before story present**: Gabriel Knox notices activity
  around Apartment 4C exceeding his existing arrangement and, out of
  self-protection rather than malice, reports it up his own contact
  chain. This is the specific human failure point that alerts Palinode.
- **Days before story present**: Elena, sensing she has very little time
  left, leaves Mara a fragmented, urgent voicemail — fragmented because
  she is speaking under real time pressure and fear of saying too much
  on a recorded line. She disappears shortly after. Julian Vale
  personally directs her move to a controlled, non-violent containment
  site, using a small, trusted, long-term team.
- **Story present, Part I**: Mara's investigation begins. She interviews
  the seven witnesses and, at the Part I climax, finds Elena's hidden
  cache and the envelope in Apartment 4C.
- **Story present, Part II, ~60–70% mark**: Mara's investigation gets
  close enough to alarm Palinode. Palinode-linked pressure reaches
  Adrienne Shaw, who restricts Mara's professional standing and access
  — the major betrayal, born of genuine institutional and personal
  self-protection rather than long-standing conspiracy.
- **Story present, Part III, ~75% mark**: Even without institutional
  standing, Mara (with Teo Marchetti's off-the-record help) keeps
  closing in. Palinode's containment calculus grows unstable — real,
  credible danger that its non-violent preference could break — the
  all-is-lost engine.
- **Story present, climax**: Mara locates Elena's containment site.
  Using her interviewing skill against Julian Vale's own unmet need for
  her understanding, she draws out enough for a legally usable
  confrontation (Teo present). She chooses to act on Elena's judgment
  over her own fully verified evidence on which parts of Palinode's
  record to expose. Julian Vale, Palinode's leadership, its criminal
  clients, and its financial infrastructure are exposed; its
  legitimately protected identities are not, through Mara's own careful
  handling. Adrienne Shaw is confronted directly. Mara and Elena reach a
  real, imperfect reconciliation. Mara helps build Elena a new,
  consensual identity — the deliberate, chosen echo of Apartment 4C.
