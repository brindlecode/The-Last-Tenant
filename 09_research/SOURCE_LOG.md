## Entry 1 — Missing-person reporting has no mandatory waiting period

- **Claim**: There is no legally or procedurally mandated waiting
  period (24, 44, or 48 hours) before a missing-person report can be
  filed with NYPD or any U.S. law enforcement agency; this is a
  persistent media myth, not real policy.
- **Source**: NYC311, "Missing Person," https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01046
  (official NYC city government resource).
- **Access date**: 2026-08-04.
- **Confidence**: High — official city-government source, consistent
  with multiple independent secondary sources found in the same search
  pass.
- **Effect on story**: Directly resolves part of
  `00_project/risk_register.md` R-006. Mara and Teo Marchetti's initial
  procedural friction must **not** be written as police making Mara
  "wait" before a report is taken — that would both repeat a debunked
  myth (banned explicitly in
  `01_author_bible/banned_cliches.md`) and be factually wrong. The real
  friction (per this research) has to come from a different, more
  accurate source: resource allocation/case prioritization once a
  report *is* taken, jurisdiction over what counts as suspicious
  circumstances, and the limits on what a civilian (even an expert one)
  can access compared to an active detective — not an intake delay.
- **Fictionalization decision**: The manuscript will depict NYPD taking
  Elena's missing-person report promptly, with no waiting-period
  obstacle; all procedural friction between Mara and Teo Marchetti will
  be built from case-prioritization, resource, and jurisdiction realism
  instead. Recorded as a required correction to
  `04_plot/five_page_synopsis.md`'s current loose framing, to apply once
  Gate 3/4 builds these scenes in detail.

## Entry 2 — "Synthetic identity fraud" is a real, defined term

- **Claim**: "Synthetic identity fraud" is a recognized, formally
  defined category of financial crime — combining real and fabricated
  personally identifiable information to create a fictitious identity —
  tracked and defined via Federal Reserve-affiliated industry
  initiatives and referenced in FinCEN guidance; described as the
  fastest-growing category of financial crime in the U.S.
- **Source**: FedPayments Improvement (Federal Reserve-affiliated),
  "Synthetic Identity Fraud Defined,"
  https://fedpaymentsimprovement.org/strategic-initiatives/payments-security/synthetic-identity-payments-fraud/synthetic-identity-fraud-defined/ ;
  corroborated by a FinCEN alert reference in the same search pass
  (https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FinCEN-Alert-DeepFakes-Alert508FINAL.pdf).
- **Access date**: 2026-08-04.
- **Confidence**: High — Federal Reserve-affiliated primary source,
  corroborated by a federal financial-crimes-enforcement reference.
- **Effect on story**: Confirms Mara's professional vocabulary
  (`03_characters/mara_voss.md`) is grounded in a real, current
  financial-crime category, not an invented one — safe to use
  "synthetic identity fraud" as her actual job-description language.
- **Fictionalization decision**: Use the real term and its real general
  shape (combining real + fabricated PII) freely in Mara's professional
  characterization and dialogue; do not extend into any specific,
  actionable technique-level detail beyond this general definition, per
  `00_project/originality_policy.md` §4's operational-harm rule.

## Entry 3 — Civilian/private investigators work missing-persons cases independently of NYPD, not alongside active police files

- **Claim**: Private and civilian investigators in NYC missing-person
  matters typically operate either after a police case has gone cold or
  in parallel tracks initiated by family/attorneys — the pattern found is
  investigator-runs-own-inquiry, not investigator-embedded-in-active-
  police-file. No source found describing any formal mechanism by which
  a civilian (however credentialed) gets read access to an open NYPD
  detective's case file.
- **Source**: Multiple NYC private-investigation firm practice
  descriptions surveyed together (International Private Investigative
  Group, "Finding a Missing Person in New York," and NYIA's "Missing
  Persons Investigations in New York City"), cross-read against the
  NYPD's own public description of detective bureau structure
  (nyc.gov/site/nypd/bureaus/investigative/detectives.page).
- **Access date**: 2026-08-05.
- **Confidence**: Medium — consistent pattern across independent
  secondary sources, but no single authoritative primary source states
  the boundary explicitly; treat the general shape (parallel, not
  embedded) as reliable, not every specific detail.
- **Effect on story**: Confirms and sharpens the working answer in
  `00_project/gate_1_concept_validation.md`: Mara cannot be written as
  having any official channel into Teo Marchetti's case file. Her
  parallel line of inquiry has to be built entirely from her own
  professional access (property/insurance/employment records, interviews
  she conducts independently as a private citizen with a personal stake,
  same as any family member would be free to do) plus whatever Teo
  chooses, at his own discretion and professional risk, to share off the
  record. This is now the locked mechanism for R-006 — see
  `05_mystery/objective_truth_timeline.md` and
  `03_characters/investigative_counterpart.md`.
- **Fictionalization decision**: Every scene where Mara has case
  information will show its source explicitly (her own pull, a witness
  interview she conducted personally, or Teo choosing to tell her
  something he technically shouldn't) — never generic "access" to "the
  file."

## Entry 4 — SIU investigators are a legally mandated function with real but bounded authority

- **Claim**: Special Investigations Units are required by insurance-fraud
  statute in most U.S. states for carriers above a set premium volume;
  SIU investigators typically hold industry credentials (e.g., the
  Certified Insurance Fraud Investigator designation via the
  International Association of Special Investigation Units) and are
  trained/authorized to conduct recorded interviews, scene inspections,
  social-media analysis, and database searches. Their authority stops at
  referral: the most severe action an SIU can take is refer a case to
  law enforcement or a state fraud bureau — no arrest power, no subpoena
  power of their own, no compelled testimony. Surveillance rules split
  sharply at the property line: public-space observation is generally
  permitted; evidence obtained through trespass or deception on private
  property is vulnerable to being thrown out of any later proceeding.
- **Source**: LegalClarity, "What Is SIU in Insurance and How Does It
  Work" (legalclarity.org); California Department of Insurance, "Special
  Investigative Unit Regulations" (insurance.ca.gov, effective Oct. 7,
  2005, a real state regulatory text); IASIU/CIFI credential references
  cross-checked against both.
- **Access date**: 2026-08-05.
- **Confidence**: High for the statutory-mandate and referral-only-
  authority claims (corroborated by a primary state regulatory document);
  Medium for the specific surveillance/trespass framing (secondary-source
  synthesis, not a single primary citation).
- **Effect on story**: This is the single most load-bearing procedural
  fact in the book. Mara's authority is real, credentialed, and
  statutorily grounded — she is not an amateur — but it is also hard-
  bounded: she can pull records, interview willing witnesses, and build a
  pattern case, and her only actual power move is to refer what she finds
  up her own chain and, potentially, to law enforcement. She cannot
  compel Gabriel Knox, Jonah Bell, or anyone else to talk to her, cannot
  search a private space without consent or a warrant she has no standing
  to obtain, and any evidence she gathers improperly is a real liability
  to the eventual case against Palinode, not just to her personally. This
  directly grounds `risk_register.md` R-006 and gives Gate 3/4 a specific,
  checkable boundary for every scene involving her investigative method.
- **Fictionalization decision**: No scene may show Mara compelling
  testimony, searching private property without a plausible consent or
  access basis (e.g., an authorized inspection tied to her employer's
  claim, a resident voluntarily letting her in), or exercising any law-
  enforcement power. Where she needs something only police can get
  (a warrant, a compelled interview, an arrest), the story gets it
  through Teo Marchetti's discretion and jeopardy, not through Mara's own
  authority quietly expanding.

## Entry 5 — Address Confidentiality Programs ("Safe at Home") are real, but narrowly scoped to address-hiding, not identity reconstruction

- **Claim**: Every U.S. state surveyed (including multiple state
  government sources) runs some form of Address Confidentiality Program
  for survivors of domestic violence, stalking, sexual assault, and human
  trafficking. The mechanism is narrow and specific: participants receive
  a substitute address for official/government correspondence and voter
  registration, and mail is forwarded by the program to the participant's
  real address. This is explicitly one layer of protection, meant to be
  combined with a broader safety plan — it does not fabricate a new
  employment history, social network, medical history, or persona.
- **Source**: Multiple state-government program pages surveyed together
  (Ohio Secretary of State "Safe at Home"; Virginia AG's Address
  Confidentiality Program; Vermont Secretary of State "Safe at Home";
  Massachusetts ACP via mass.gov), cross-read against a Washington
  University Law Review piece on the ACP model generally. New York State
  operates an equivalent program through the NY Department of State
  (not itself separately queried this pass — flagged in
  `FACT_CHECK_QUEUE.md` to confirm NY-specific programmatic detail before
  any scene names it directly).
- **Access date**: 2026-08-05.
- **Confidence**: High for the general ACP mechanism and its narrow
  scope; Medium (unconfirmed this pass) for New York State's specific
  program name and procedural detail.
- **Effect on story**: This is a genuinely useful and clarifying fact for
  the book's own moral architecture, not just texture. It sharpens
  exactly why Palinode exists and where its clients' real alternative
  fails them: a legitimate ACP hides an address, full stop. It does not
  build Evelyn Price a nurse who genuinely remembers "Lena Hart," or a
  neighbor's confident testimony, or a documented pregnancy history —
  the manufactured-continuity layer that makes Palinode's version
  dangerous and effective is specifically the part no real program
  offers. Confirms and strengthens Gate 1's premise that Palinode fills a
  real, specific gap ("official protection was too narrow") rather than
  duplicating something that already exists legitimately.
- **Fictionalization decision**: At least one scene (recommended: a
  Jonah Bell or Evelyn Price beat, Gate 4 to place) should let a
  character reference or consider a real-shaped legitimate resource by
  its general description (a state address-confidentiality program) and
  find it insufficient for their specific danger — establishing, without
  a villain monologue, exactly why the manufactured-continuity escalation
  felt necessary to them. Do not use Palinode as an implied replacement
  for or better version of real address-confidentiality programs in any
  way that could read as discouraging a real reader from using one — the
  book's frame must stay "this fictional network does something no real
  program does, at a terrible hidden cost," never "real programs aren't
  enough, so do this instead."

## Entry 6 — Skip tracing and data-broker work are legal, regulated, real professions with a tightening compliance environment

- **Claim**: Skip tracing (locating people via records aggregation) is
  legal and used across debt collection, real estate, process serving,
  and insurance. It is regulated indirectly through the Fair Credit
  Reporting Act and Fair Debt Collection Practices Act; a minority of
  states (California, Vermont, named specifically) require data brokers
  to register. The regulatory environment is actively tightening —
  registered data brokers face new consumer-deletion-request processing
  requirements on a recurring cadence beginning August 1, 2026.
- **Source**: USInfoSearch, "What is Skip Tracing? A Comprehensive Guide"
  (usinfosearch.com); Federal Register, "Protecting Americans From
  Harmful Data Broker Practices (Regulation V)," 2024-28690
  (federalregister.gov, a primary federal regulatory document).
- **Access date**: 2026-08-05.
- **Confidence**: High — corroborated by a primary Federal Register
  document for the regulatory-tightening claim specifically.
- **Effect on story**: Grounds Calvin Moss's profession as a real,
  legitimate, if ethically gray-capable field, and gives Gate 3 a
  concrete, current detail to use: the tightening compliance landscape
  (the August 2026 deletion-cadence requirement lands almost exactly at
  the story's present moment) is a plausible, specific, non-magical
  reason Calvin's own professional footing — and Palinode's reliance on
  data-broker-sourced records — would be feeling new pressure right now,
  independent of anything Mara or Elena does. Usable as background
  texture explaining part of "why now" (`risk_register.md` R-009)
  without requiring it to carry that answer alone.
- **Fictionalization decision**: Calvin Moss's work is legal, regulated
  skip-tracing/records work; where his history with Palinode-adjacent
  clients is discussed, keep it in the register of "legitimate-seeming
  freelance client, ambiguous end use" rather than any implication that
  the profession itself is inherently criminal.

## Entry 7 — Soviet Jewish emigration to Brighton Beach, Brooklyn, 1975–1980

- **Claim**: A well-documented wave of roughly 40,000 Soviet Jewish
  émigrés settled in Brighton Beach, Brooklyn between 1975 and 1980,
  driven by loosened Soviet emigration policy and persistent state
  antisemitism/religious restriction. The neighborhood became known as
  "Little Odessa." Soviet-trained professional credentials were
  routinely not recognized on arrival in the U.S., a common experience
  requiring retraining or career change.
- **Source**: The Times of Israel, "In Brooklyn's 'Little Odesa,' Jews
  from Ukraine and Russia find war 'terrifying'"
  (timesofisrael.com); The Science Survey, "Brighton Beach: The Soviet
  Jewish Refuge in Brooklyn"; New York Public Library blog, "From
  Odessa to New York City: The Unique Legacy of America's Soviet Jewry"
  (nypl.org); Brooklyn Jewish Historical Initiative, "Brighton Beach"
  neighborhood page (brooklynjewish.org) — cross-read together.
- **Access date**: 2026-08-05.
- **Confidence**: High — corroborated across multiple independent
  sources including a major cultural institution (NYPL) and a dedicated
  historical initiative.
- **Effect on story**: Replaces the prior generic "emigrated from
  Russia, late 1970s/early 1980s, working assumption" placeholder in
  `03_characters/irena_petrov.md` with a specific, real, respectfully
  documented historical anchor — directly satisfies her file's own
  standing instruction not to invent cultural specifics from assumption.
- **Fictionalization decision**: Irena Petrov's backstory now specifies
  Odesa (Ukrainian SSR) as her city of origin, 1978 as her arrival year,
  initial settlement in Brighton Beach, and a real, common credential-
  non-recognition experience (mathematics teacher retrained as a
  bookkeeper) — all consistent with, not contradicting, this documented
  wave. Her specific family circumstances (husband Mikhail, a brother
  left behind) remain invented detail layered onto this real historical
  frame, not presented as documented fact themselves.
