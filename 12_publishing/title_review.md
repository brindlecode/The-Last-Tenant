# Title Review

STATUS: DRAFT — genuine Gate 1 stress-test, usable now; final
confirmation deferred to pre-submission.

## Current title: THE LAST TENANT

## Strengths

- Directly evocative of the premise (tenancy, occupancy, Apartment 4C)
  and of the identity theme (who gets to be the "last," i.e., final/real,
  version of a person).
- Short, commercial, genre-appropriate rhythm — reads correctly next to
  other psychological-thriller titles without imitating a specific one.
- Works on a second read once the reader knows the ending: "the last
  tenant" can retroactively mean several different people or facts
  depending on how the ending resolves, which is a good property in a
  title for this kind of book.

## Weaknesses / risks

- **Not unique.** Flagged in `00_project/risk_register.md` R-013: this
  same repository's rejected prior branch used the identical title for
  an unrelated novel, and a related project by the same author used the
  near-identical "The Silent Tenant." "[The] Last Tenant"/"[The] Silent
  Tenant"-style titles are also a common pattern across the wider
  domestic-suspense market generally (a title-structure convention, not
  a specific collision confirmed by this session's research — a real
  marketplace search is still needed, see below). This is a
  discoverability risk, not a legal one — titles aren't protectable IP.
- Doesn't signal the book's specific differentiator (the seven-witness
  mechanism, the identity-engineering premise) — a reader scanning
  titles alone might expect a more conventional missing-tenant mystery.

## Alternatives considered (quick pass, not exhaustive — full title
brainstorm is a `12_publishing/`-stage exercise once the manuscript
exists and can be titled against its actual finished text)

- *Seven Names for Elena* — signals the differentiator directly; risks
  sounding more literary/less thriller-commercial than intended.
- *The Continuity* — thematically precise (Palinode's own term) but
  abstract, may not read as a thriller title on sight.
- *Apartment 4C* — concrete and intriguing, but flat/generic without
  context.
- *Bellwether* — strong single-word title, but "bellwether" as a word
  doesn't obviously connote a residential building to most readers
  without the "House," which is a lot of title to carry alone.

## Recommendation

**Keep "THE LAST TENANT" as the working title through drafting** — very
real cost to renaming now across the entire built repository, and the
uniqueness problem is a marketing concern, not a drafting one. **Do not
finalize it as the publication title without an actual marketplace title
search** (Amazon/major retailer listings, Goodreads, recent publishing
industry announcements) at the pre-submission stage — see
`submission_checklist.md`.
