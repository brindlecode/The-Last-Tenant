# Gate 0 — Premise & Concept Lock

Status: **COMPLETE**

## Title

**THE LAST TENANT**

Double meaning, both literal by the end of the novel: (1) Cass Ferris, the
tenant who held Nora's flat before her, and the question of what "last"
means for her — last in the sequence, or last in the sense of final; (2) a
promise that whoever holds the truth about Sovereign Mill next may not get
to be a tenant there at all — may not get to leave the way tenants are
supposed to.

## Logline

A newly separated building surveyor takes a suspiciously cheap lease in a
converted mill and finds the previous tenant never really left — she's still
in the wiring, the paperwork, and the landlord's too-careful smile — and the
closer Nora gets to the truth of where Cass Ferris went, the more Sovereign
Mill starts treating her like the same kind of problem.

## Genre & comparable titles

Domestic / psychological suspense with a fair-play mystery engine and a
closed-setting (single building) cast. British-set. Tonally adjacent to
*The Silent Patient* (structural misdirection, a protagonist whose expertise
is the investigative engine), *Behind Closed Doors* (a charming, controlling
man hiding something at the center of a social circle), and *Verity*
(discovered documents as a second, dangerous narrative thread). Distinct
from those comps in its engine: the mystery is solved through *building
forensics* — planning records, fire-safety paperwork, physical fabric of a
structure — not journalism, therapy transcripts, or a manuscript.

## Core reader promise

- A single closed setting (Sovereign Mill) the reader will know as well as
  their own building by the end — every hallway, every locked door earns its
  geography.
- A mystery solvable in principle from the physical and paper evidence Nora
  finds, fairly seeded, never solved by coincidence or a confession that
  comes from nowhere.
- Dread that escalates through hospitality, not violence, for most of the
  first two parts — the horror of a landlord who is *too* attentive, a
  building that is *too* well maintained, a helpfulness that never quite
  lets you say no.
- A protagonist whose professional competence is real and useful (she is
  never rescued by luck she didn't earn), and whose personal collapse
  (marriage, home, financial footing) is never resolved by the plot as a
  consolation prize — it has its own arc.

## Central dramatic question

*What happened to Cass Ferris — and is Sovereign Mill going to let Nora find
out, or become the answer to the same question?*

Underneath it, the novel's real question: what does it cost to keep
insisting a home is safe when the evidence says otherwise — and who gets to
decide when it's time to leave.

## Themes

- **Tenancy as vulnerability.** To rent is to be provisional, inspectable,
  disposable-by-contract — and Nora, mid-divorce, is living that precarity
  for the first time as an adult, in a building where it turns out to be
  literally true for someone else.
- **Expertise as both armor and blindness.** Nora's trained eye for a
  building's lies is exactly what makes her dangerous to Sovereign Mill —
  and exactly what she used, for years, not to look too closely at her own
  marriage.
- **Hospitality as a control strategy.** Julian Cole's charm is not a mask
  that slips to reveal menace; the charm *is* the menace, functioning
  correctly, the entire time.
- **What a building is willing to hide inside its own walls**, literally and
  administratively — the gap between what a certificate says and what a
  structure actually is.

## Tone & register

Close third person, past tense, single POV (Nora) for the whole of Part I
(see `registers/voice-style-guide.md` for full mechanics). Atmosphere builds
through precise, professional noticing rather than gothic excess — dread
delivered in the register of a survey report that's just started to
contradict itself. Restrained, controlled prose; the building is never
described as "creepy," it is described accurately, and accuracy is what's
frightening.

## Series / scope note

Written as a standalone. Structured in three parts (~30–34 chapters total,
see Gate 4). This project drafts Part I only in the current phase.
