# Gate 1 — World & Setting Bible

Status: **COMPLETE**

## World rules

- Contemporary, real-world Britain, lightly fictionalized: the city, its
  streets, and every business in it are invented. No real cities, streets,
  brands, or public figures are used or implied.
- No supernatural elements. Every uncanny effect in the novel has a material,
  discoverable cause. Atmosphere is built from architecture, administration,
  and behavior — never from anything the physical world can't produce.
- Year is never pinned to a real calendar year or tied to real datable news
  events. Season and relative chronology (tracked in
  `registers/timeline-register.md`) are the only time anchors.
- Story opens in **mid-to-late November**, running into winter across Part
  I. Short days, early dark, rain turning to hard cold — weather is used
  deliberately, not decoratively (see continuity register for the running
  weather log).

## Rookford

Invented mid-size post-industrial British city on the river **Fenn**.
Once a milling and rope-making center; textile and maritime-supply money
built its Victorian core, most of which emptied out through the later 20th
century and is now mid-gentrification. Rookford has the shape of a
composite Northern/Western English city — canal spurs, red-brick railway
viaducts, a hilltop older town above the flood-prone river flats — without
mapping onto any single real city. Treat it as fixed once used; do not
introduce real Rookford geography from any actual British city.

### Marsh Wharf

The district along the Fenn's north bank where the old rope- and
sailcloth-works stood. Cobbled yards, converted warehouses, a working canal
basin now half marina/half derelict. Gentrifying for about a decade:
converted-loft flats, two craft breweries, a bouldering gym in a former
sail loft, and — the one holdout of the old economy — a working scrap-metal
yard across the water that the new residents complain about at council
meetings. Marsh Wharf floods at the margins in bad winters; the mill itself
sits on slightly raised ground and does not flood, a fact the marketing
brochure mentions more than once.

### Key locations (continuity-locked; also see `continuity-register.md`)

- **Sovereign Mill** — the novel's central setting. Ropewalk Lane, Marsh
  Wharf, Rookford. Full building bible below.
- **Ropewalk Lane** — the cobbled lane the mill fronts onto, named for the
  original ropewalk (the long straight lane where rope was twisted by hand)
  that ran here into the 1950s. Narrow, cobbled, no through traffic.
- **The Ropewalk Café** — small independent café at the Marsh Wharf end of
  the lane, in a former weighing-house. Nora's default coffee/work spot;
  where she meets Priya in Rookford.
  Owner/barista on page: **Sam** (minor, recurring, last name not needed).
- **Hollis & Vane, Chartered Surveyors** — Nora's employer, offices in
  Rookford's business district (the old Corn Exchange building), a
  15-minute cycle or bus ride from Marsh Wharf.
- **Aldermoor Road, Percy Park** — the tree-lined street where the house
  Nora and Marcus owned together still stands; a 20-minute drive from Marsh
  Wharf. Sold as part of the separation; new owners have already repainted
  the door, which Nora has seen once, by accident, and never mentions
  wanting to see again.
- **Whitlock & Bryce** — family law firm where Priya works, Rookford city
  centre. Not Nora's own solicitor's firm — Priya is a friend, deliberately
  kept off the record of Nora's actual case.

## Sovereign Mill — building bible

Built **1868** as a sailcloth and rope mill for **Sovereign & Tait
Ropeworks**; five storeys of load-bearing brick around a cast-iron frame,
long mill windows, a full-height loading bay with an original timber-and-
iron hoist beam preserved as a feature. Ropeworks operations wound down in
the 1950s; light industrial and storage tenants kept it alive, badly
maintained, until it stood mostly empty through the 1990s–2000s. Bought and
converted to residential 2016–2018 by **Julian Cole**, trading as
**Sovereign Living** — Cole's own management company, which lets and
manages every unit directly rather than through a third-party agency. This
is unusual for a building this size and is a fact Nora, professionally,
clocks early as slightly odd rather than sinister.

Eleven let flats plus Cole's own unit. No external property manager, no
residents' committee — Sovereign Living, meaning functionally Cole and
Desmond Pye, runs everything.

### Floor by floor

- **Ground floor**: entrance lobby (original hoist-beam feature retained
  overhead); post/buzzer panel for all eleven flats plus "THE LOFT"; the old
  loading bay converted into a residents' lounge (rarely used); bike store;
  door through to the courtyard. **Desmond Pye's flat** is off the lobby,
  in what was the ropewalk foreman's office — he is the only live-in staff.
- **1st floor**: Flats 1–3. Small shared gym and laundry room (original
  hoist-shaft chase now boxed in and used as a service riser here).
- **2nd floor**: Flats 4–6. **Flat 6 is Nora's** (Cass Ferris's before her).
  **Flat 5 is Rena Okafor's**, directly across the landing from Flat 6.
  Flat 4 is owned (not rented) by an absentee owner who is almost never
  present — mentioned, not on page in Part I.
- **3rd floor**: Flats 7–9.
- **4th floor**: Flats 10–11. **Flat 10** has been "mid-renovation" —
  officially uninhabitable, officially empty — since before Nora's tenancy
  began; this cover story does not hold up to scrutiny and is a Part I
  engine (see Gate 3). The **Plant Room** (mechanical/electrical
  distribution, fire alarm panel, sprinkler control valves) is also on this
  floor, beside the boxed-in top of the original hoist shaft: an
  unmarked-except-for-one-sign steel door, keyed, "PLANT ROOM — AUTHORISED
  ACCESS ONLY."
- **5th floor / roof**: **The Loft** — Julian Cole's own flat, the full
  footprint of the top floor — and residents' access to a shared roof
  terrace.

### Vertical circulation

- **The Hoist** — the original goods hoist, retained and re-motorized as a
  slow, characterful lift with a lattice concertina gate. Serves all floors.
  Loud, deliberate, impossible to use unnoticed.
  - Fair-play note: this matters. Anyone using the Hoist can be heard
    building-wide, but the original stone stair (see below) cannot.
- **The original stair** — stone treads, iron balustrade, enclosed during
  conversion behind a lightweight stud-and-plasterboard partition rather
  than rebuilt to full fire-rated separation. Quiet underfoot compared to
  the Hoist. This detail matters to Gate 3 and must never be explained
  on the page in Part I beyond what a surveyor would notice and privately
  flag — see `bible/03-mystery-architecture.md` and
  `registers/prohibited-revelations.md`.

### Textures reserved for this building (use consistently)

Exposed brick painted rather than stripped bare in the corridors (Cole's
brief called it "warmth"); original cast-iron columns left load-bearing and
visible inside several flats, including Flat 6; mill windows single-glazed
behind slim secondary glazing units, which mist at the edges in cold
weather; corridor carpet a specific dark bottle-green (chosen by Cole
personally, mentioned in Sovereign Living's marketing copy); the smell of
the building is cold stone and fresh paint over something mineral
underneath, like wet pennies, strongest near the stairwell.

## Sensory palette (Part I)

Cold, wet, early dark. Rain on single-glazed mill windows. The Hoist's
mechanical clank as a building-wide clock. Fresh paint smell that shouldn't
still be fresh. Bottle-green carpet. The specific quiet of a well-built old
building that has been *made* quieter in one direction only.
