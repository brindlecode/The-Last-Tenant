# Gate 2 — Character Bible

Status: **COMPLETE**

Each profile includes a **knowledge-state at story start** line. This is the
control against which the clue and prohibited-revelations audits are run —
a character can only act on what their knowledge-state permits.

---

## Nora Hale — protagonist, POV

**Eleanor "Nora" Hale, 34.** Chartered building surveyor, Associate at
Hollis & Vane. Separated (not yet divorced) from Marcus Hale after eleven
years married. No children — two pregnancies lost, the second eighteen
months before the novel opens; this is backstory, referenced obliquely,
never staged as a flashback scene in Part I.

**Physical**: Tall, rangy, dresses for site visits more than offices — good
boots, practical nails, a surveyor's habit of standing with weight balanced
like she's testing a floor. Dark blonde hair kept short enough to forget
about. A scar through one eyebrow from a scaffolding accident at 26 that she
tells a boring, true story about when asked.

**Voice**: Precise, dry, under-reacts out loud and over-reacts on the page
(i.e., in her interiority, which is our access to her). Thinks in
professional categories under stress — defect, load path, remedial cost —
as a control mechanism. Sarcasm is her tell for pain.

**Want (surface)**: A stable, private place to be no one's wife, no one's
case study, for a while.
**Want (true)**: To be competent at something again after a year of feeling
professionally sharp and personally catastrophic — to trust her own
judgment again, specifically about people, not just buildings.
**Wound**: Marcus remortgaged their house against a "sure thing" investment
with his brother without telling her until it had already gone bad; she
found out from a letter, not from him. The house sold at a loss to cover
it. She is furious less at the money than at being *managed* — kept
comfortable and uninformed in her own home, by someone she'd have said knew
her better than anyone. This is the emotional engine that makes her
recognize the same shape (comfortable, uninformed, managed) at Sovereign
Mill before she can prove it.
**Fear**: That her judgment can't be trusted — that she'll either miss the
real thing again or invent a crisis out of a marriage-shaped hole in her
life. (Fair-play unreliability seed: Nora doubts herself audibly and often;
the reader should be able to worry she's right to doubt herself, right up
until the physical evidence stops being deniable.)

**Skills that function as plot engine**: Reads a building's fabric the way
others read faces. Can access planning applications, building control
records, and party-wall/structural filings through professional databases
and public registers. Knows the vocabulary to ask the right question of the
right official — and knows exactly how those questions look from the
outside: like someone who can't let go of something that isn't her business.

**Knowledge-state at story start (Ch.1 open)**: Knows nothing about Cass
Ferris beyond a name on a lease clause ("outgoing tenant"). Does not know
Julian Cole beyond his role as landlord. Has no reason yet to connect her
own situation to anyone else's.

## Julian Cole — landlord, developer, antagonist

**Julian Cole, 44.** Owner-developer of Sovereign Mill, sole director of
Sovereign Living. Self-made: grew up in Marsh Wharf when it was still
working docks and scrap yards, made his first money in commercial-to-
residential conversions elsewhere in Rookford before Sovereign Mill, which
is personal to him in a way his other projects weren't — he talks about it
like a marriage, not a portfolio asset.

**Physical**: Compact, precise, dresses like he's always about to be
photographed for a local business supplement — no tie, good coat, a watch
that's expensive without being loud. Makes strong eye contact a beat too
long. Remembers everyone's name, coffee order, moving-in date.

**Presentation vs. truth**: Presents as the rare hands-on landlord who
actually cares — personally welcomes new tenants, personally handles
maintenance requests, personally remembers birthdays. This is not
performance layered over indifference; the attentiveness is real *and* it
is a control mechanism, simultaneously, without contradiction — that's the
character's engine. He needs to know everything happening inside his
building because the building's paper safety record does not match its
physical reality, and "caring landlord" is the version of surveillance
tenants thank him for.

**Voice**: Warm, specific, flattering in ways that land because they're
accurate (he has clearly paid attention). Never raises his voice on the
page in Part I. Redirects rather than refuses — a question he doesn't want
answered gets met with a better question back, or a genuinely charming
non-sequitur, never a visible stonewall.

**True motive (Gate 3 — confidential, not for reader confirmation in Part
I)**: The 2016–18 conversion cut structural and fire-safety corners to hit
a financing deadline — see `03-mystery-architecture.md`. Full remediation
would cost more than Cole has, would be criminally actionable, and would
end Sovereign Living and his other developments by reputation alone. Cass
Ferris, researching a piece on post-conversion fire safety, found the
specific irregularity (the enclosed original stair, see Gate 1) and was
building a documented case. What Cole did about that is Gate 3 material and
is not established on the page in Chapters 1–4.

**Knowledge-state at story start**: Knows exactly what happened to Cass
Ferris. Knows Nora is a surveyor before she tells him — it's on her
tenancy application, which he reads personally, which is itself the
in-world explanation for how he "already knows" things about her that
unsettle her; this must read as explicable-but-uncomfortable in Part I, not
supernatural.

## Desmond Pye — caretaker

**Desmond Pye, 61.** Lives in the ground-floor caretaker's flat. Worked
general maintenance and security at Marsh Wharf light-industrial sites from
his twenties; his father worked the ropewalk itself in its last years
before 1950s closure, so Pye has generational memory of the building's
original bones that predates the conversion drawings. Cole hired him as
caretaker on day one of the conversion and has kept him on since — Pye
knows the building better than anyone alive, including Cole.

**Physical**: Heavyset, slow-moving, hands permanently marked from fifty
years of manual work. Keeps a cardigan on even in the boiler-warm lobby.

**Voice**: Understated, dry, answers the question he was asked and not the
one underneath it — until pressed, at which point he says more true things
faster than he means to, then retreats into small talk. Genuinely fond of
tenants; genuinely loyal to Cole in a complicated, decades-deep way that
isn't simple obedience.

**Function**: The character who *could* tell Nora almost everything and
mostly doesn't, not because he's protecting Cole out of malice but out of a
tangle of loyalty, fear for his own position and flat, and grief he hasn't
processed about Cass specifically (he liked her; he misses her; he has not
let himself think hard about why she stopped visiting him for tea on
Sunday afternoons). His slips are real slips, not withheld exposition —
when he gives Nora something in Chapter 4, it should cost him visibly.

**Knowledge-state at story start**: Knows more than Cole realizes he knows
about the building's fire-safety compromise (it's his job to know the
building). Does not know the full truth of what happened to Cass — has
suspicions he has actively avoided completing into a conclusion. This
distinction (partial, avoided knowledge vs. Cole's complete knowledge) must
stay clean across every scene he's in.

## Rena Okafor — neighbor

**Rena Okafor, 31.** Freelance illustrator, two years in Flat 5, directly
across the landing from Nora. Rents because she likes the flexibility, not
out of hardship — a useful contrast to Nora's and Cass's more precarious
reasons for being tenants.

**Physical**: Paint-stained hands she's stopped apologizing for, a rotation
of oversized shirts, moves through the building like she owns the
confidence of it even though she owns none of the equity.

**Voice**: Warm, funny, a talker — the character who delivers information
through gossip and genuine friendliness rather than interrogation, so
exposition through her never feels like exposition. Notices people more
than buildings (contrast to Nora).

**Relationship to Cass**: Friendly hallway-and-occasional-wine-night
neighbor, not a close friend. Has one of Cass's jumpers, borrowed and never
returned, which she still feels faintly guilty and weird about keeping. Was
mildly hurt, more than worried, when Cass stopped answering texts — read it
as ghosting, the way any friendship can end, until Nora's questions start
making her recalculate in real time across Part I.

**Knowledge-state at story start**: Believes the "moved out suddenly, a bit
stressed, people do that" story, because she has never had a reason not to.
Has small true details (the jumper; a changed, anxious Cass in her last few
weeks; a night Cass mentioned "a stupid work thing about the stairs" and
laughed it off) that are real clues she doesn't know she's holding.

## Cass Ferris — the last tenant (pre-story; appears via traces only in
Part I: documents, texts glimpsed, secondhand report, never in-scene/
present-day)

**Cassandra "Cass" Ferris, 28** at the time she held Flat 6. Freelance
journalist, Rookford-based, building a reported piece on fast-tracked
post-conversion fire-safety sign-offs across the city — Sovereign Mill was
one of several buildings in her notes, not originally her main target,
until she moved in as a tenant herself and started noticing things about
her own building.

**Voice (as it survives in documents/texts)**: Quick, informal, uses humor
to undercut how worried she actually is — a register Nora starts to
recognize as familiar, uncomfortably close to her own.

**Function in Part I**: Present only as residue — forwarded mail, a
notebook fragment, a still-logged-in portal session, secondhand testimony
from Rena and Pye, a buzzer sticker. She must feel like a specific person
accumulating in fragments, not a plot device. No Part I scene confirms
whether she is alive, dead, relocated under duress, or in hiding; all of it
must remain fairly, genuinely ambiguous.

**Knowledge-state (backstory, frozen at her disappearance)**: Had assembled
a partial but real dossier connecting Sovereign Mill's fire-safety sign-off
to an approved inspector she believed had a financial relationship with
Cole. Had not yet published or filed anything. Had confided *some* of this
to someone before she vanished — who, and how much, is Gate 3 material.

## Marcus Hale — estranged husband

**Marcus Hale, 36.** Off-page for all of Part I except calls, texts, and
one voicemail; no in-person scene in Chapters 1–4. Not a villain — the
novel is explicit (via Nora's own fairness toward him, which is a
characterization choice for her, not softness in the narrative) that he
made one catastrophic, controlling decision and has been sorry, uselessly,
ever since. His presence in Part I is entirely about unfinished admin
(house completion paperwork, a joint account, a forwarding-address
argument) that keeps dragging Nora back into the marriage's unfinished
business at the worst possible moments.

**Function**: Emotional counter-rhythm and red-herring texture — his
neediness and Nora's guilt about him are real and must never resolve into
"he was secretly involved at Sovereign Mill too"; that would cheapen both
plots. He stays exactly what he is: the wound she's still bandaging while a
new wound opens.

## Priya Chandrasekaran — best friend

**Priya Chandrasekaran, 35.** Family law solicitor at Whitlock & Bryce,
Rookford. Nora's closest friend since university; deliberately *not* Nora's
own divorce solicitor. Grounded, funny, low patience for Nora
self-sacrificing or catastrophizing, but takes her seriously — Priya is the
character who can say "are you doing the thing" and also say "no, that's
real, tell me again" in the same conversation, which lets the novel
interrogate Nora's reliability out loud without a scene ever fully
resolving it either way in Part I.

**Knowledge-state**: Only what Nora tells her, by phone/text/occasional
coffee. Has no independent access to Sovereign Mill.

## Relationship map (Part I state)

- Nora ↔ Marcus: legally still married, practically separated, unfinished
  admin, guilt on both sides.
- Nora ↔ Julian Cole: landlord/tenant, escalating from professional warmth
  to Nora's private unease; Cole is managing her attentively without her
  full awareness of why.
- Nora ↔ Desmond Pye: growing tentative trust; he is the character most
  likely to give her real information, at real cost to himself.
- Nora ↔ Rena: fast, easy new-neighbor friendship; Rena is Nora's primary
  in-building social anchor and unwitting source.
- Nora ↔ Priya: established, safe, off-site — the friendship that lets
  Nora say things out loud.
- Cole ↔ Pye: employer/employee with twenty years of history underneath it;
  loyalty complicated by what Pye half-knows.
- Cass (backstory) ↔ Pye: Cass was fond of Pye and he of her; her silence
  toward him after she "left" is a detail Rena knows and mentions before
  its weight is clear.
