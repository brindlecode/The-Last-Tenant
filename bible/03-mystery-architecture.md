# Gate 3 — Mystery Architecture

Status: **COMPLETE** · **CONFIDENTIAL — PRODUCTION DOCUMENT, NOT READER-FACING**

This file must never be summarized, quoted, or leaked into `manuscript/`.
Its purpose is to let every clue placed in the prose be planted against a
truth that is already fixed, so nothing is retconned and nothing is
accidentally revealed early. Cross-check every chapter against
`registers/prohibited-revelations.md`, which is the enforceable, per-chapter
version of the rules below.

## The truth

Cass Ferris did not move out of Flat 6 voluntarily. She died in Sovereign
Mill on the night of **Thursday 14 March** (see `timeline-register.md` for
full backstory dating), roughly eight months before Nora's tenancy begins.

**What happened:** Cass had spent several months researching a freelance
piece on fast-tracked fire-safety sign-offs at converted buildings across
Rookford, Sovereign Mill among several examples — before she ever
considered living there. She moved into Flat 6 in January in part *because*
of the story: proximity, access, a chance to document the building from
inside. By March she had narrowed her focus to Sovereign Mill specifically
and had identified the defect: during the 2016–18 conversion, the original
stone stair was enclosed behind a lightweight stud partition instead of
being rebuilt to full fire-rated separation, and the "approved inspector"
who signed off building control for the conversion — a private inspector
Cole paid directly, a legal but conflict-prone arrangement — had a prior
undisclosed financial relationship with Cole's earlier developments. Neither
fact alone is necessarily fraud; together, documented, they were enough to
trigger a council investigation and force remediation Cole could not afford
without collapsing Sovereign Living and his other developments.

Cass told Desmond Pye a partial version of what she'd found — she liked
him, trusted him, and needed to know the building's real pre-conversion
structure to be sure of her reading of the drawings. Pye, uneasy but not
understanding the stakes, confirmed the stair had indeed just been "boxed
in, not rebuilt" — true, offered as an old man's shrug, not a confession.

Cole learned Cass was close to something specific (not from Pye directly —
Cole reads every resident's post and portal activity as a matter of habit
and control, and noticed her building-control correspondence). He
approached her the evening of March 14th, in the fourth-floor corridor near
the Plant Room, with an offer: a buyout, framed as a "relocation
assistance" payment, generous, in exchange for dropping the Sovereign Mill
angle of her story. She refused, told him she already had enough
documented to be dangerous to him even without more, and said she was
leaving to file a complaint that night. He grabbed her arm to stop her from
leaving with what she was carrying (her bag, containing note materials).
She pulled away, hard, on the top step of the original stair, at the point
where the modern partition meets the old stone — and fell. The fall killed
her. **This was not premeditated murder. It was a physical restraint that
became a fatal accident during an attempt to stop her leaving with
evidence — manslaughter in substance, and Cole's choice to conceal it
afterward is the deliberate crime that follows.**

**The cover-up:** Cole moved her body that night and over the following
week concealed it within Flat 10, then mid-renovation and easy to seal
further under cover of "structural remediation" — a fact he was already
uncomfortably able to justify to himself, since Flat 10 also needed work to
quietly correct the very stair-enclosure defect Cass had found, before any
inspection could catch it. He had Pye carry out the physical building work
(stud wall, subfloor, skim and paint) over several night sessions, telling
Pye only that it was urgent, discreet insurance-related remedial work nobody
should discuss — a plausible-enough instruction from an employer Pye had
trusted for twenty years that Pye did it without demanding the full reason,
and has not let himself reconstruct the timing since. Cole took Cass's
phone and personal bag from the scene; used her phone over the following
72 hours to send a handful of texts to Rena, her landlord contact, and her
editor establishing a "sudden change of plans, needs space, will be in
touch" narrative, then destroyed it. He had Flat 6 professionally deep-
cleaned and had a small section of the party wall with the stairwell
(behind where Cass's desk had stood) opened and reclosed to retrieve a
folder of her printed notes she'd hidden there — done in haste, by Pye,
without full explanation, which is the source of the amateur patch and
fresh paint Nora notices in Chapter 1.

Separately, and with no connection to that retrieval, a corner had torn off
one page of Cass's own working notebook weeks earlier, in the ordinary
course of her using it at the desk she kept against that same wall — the
kind of small tear that happens to a notebook carried everywhere — and had
gone unnoticed under the loose skirting since. The cleaners never found it.
This is the fragment Nora finds in Chapter 1: mundane in origin, planted
long before the night of March 14th, and for that reason all the more
genuinely ambiguous when Nora finds it — nothing about how it got there
implies anything happened, only that Cass sat and wrote at that exact spot.

The notebook itself (distinctive burnt-orange cover, spiral bound, "C.F."
in silver paint pen on the front, one page still short a torn corner) was
in her bag when she died. Cole brought the bag into Flat 10 with the body
that night — he needed what was in it before he could be sure of anything
— and stripped it there: phone kept out for the fake-texts scheme, papers
and the notebook set aside on the floor while he worked. In the days that
followed, with Pye's genuine renovation debris accumulating in the same
room as cover, the notebook was swept up with a load of offcuts and rubble
bags during a contractor changeover and never missed — an accident of
housekeeping on Cole's part, not a plan; the single loose thread in an
otherwise controlled cover-up, and the mechanism for the Chapter 4 act-out.

**Cass Ferris is dead.** This fact is fixed for the writers' use now; it is
never to be confirmed, shown, or made the only possible reading on the page
during Part I. Ambiguity (alive-in-hiding vs. dead) must remain genuinely
sustainable through Chapter 4 and beyond.

## Who knows what (authoritative; matches Gate 2 knowledge-states)

- **Julian Cole**: Knows everything above, completely.
- **Desmond Pye**: Knows the stair was "boxed in, not rebuilt" (told Cass
  this himself, thought nothing of it at the time). Knows he did unexplained
  urgent building work in Flat 6 and Flat 10 in the days after Cass "left."
  Has never let himself line these facts up. Does not know, and has
  actively avoided finding out, what happened the night of March 14th.
- **Rena Okafor**: Knows only ordinary-neighbor-level facts (see Gate 2);
  no knowledge of the stair defect, the notebook, or the cover-up.
- **Nora Hale**: By end of Chapter 4, has: the torn notebook-page corner
  (Ch.1); a glimpsed, partial portal notification (Ch.2); Rena's anecdotes
  (Ch.2–3); public planning/building-control irregularities (Ch.3); Cole
  naming Cass unprompted (Ch.3); Pye's "fourth floor" slip (Ch.4); and the
  matching notebook itself, found in Flat 10 (Ch.4). She does **not** have,
  and must not be written as having, any confirmation of Cole's guilt,
  Cass's fate, or the stairwell defect's technical specifics.

### Red herrings (deliberate, tracked)

- **Marcus** — emotional/genre-reflex misdirection only (reader wariness of
  "the husband"). No planted evidence ever implicates him in Sovereign Mill.
  Keep his subplot mechanically separate at all times.
- **Rena** (the jumper; a friendship that "just ended") — soft suspicion,
  defused by consistent, transparent characterization by Ch.3.
- **Clue #5** (construction shortcuts) — true but incomplete; reads like it
  could be the whole mystery (developer fraud/cost-cutting) when it's the
  motive-generating backdrop for something worse.

## Fair-play check

Every fact Nora uses to reach a conclusion by end of Chapter 4 is either (a)
something she physically finds, (b) public record she is professionally
qualified to pull, or (c) something a character with no motive to lie tells
her. No clue depends on coincidence beyond the ordinary coincidence of
tenancy (she happens to live in Flat 6) established as premise, not
plot-resolving luck. Cole's and Pye's behavior is consistent with their
Gate 2 profiles in every scene — Cole never slips into villain-speak; Pye's
one slip is costly and immediately regretted on the page.

## Prohibited revelations — Chapters 1–4 (enforced per-chapter in
`registers/prohibited-revelations.md`)

1. Whether Cass is alive or dead.
2. Cole's causal role in her death.
3. The location or existence of Cass's remains.
4. That Pye performed concealment construction work (his *unexplained* work
   may be gestured at only as far as his own Ch.4 slip permits).
5. The technical specifics of the stair fire-safety defect (Nora may sense
   "something professionally wrong," never named in narration or dialogue).
6. The existence/contents of Cass's full dossier, or who she told beyond
   the fair-play clues listed above.
7. The contents of the unopened Building Control Partnership envelope.
8. Cole's financial motive/exposure — never narrated; he is not a POV
   character and his interiority is never accessed.

## Reveal schedule beyond Part I (high-level; not detailed chapter work
in this drafting phase)

- **Part II**: Nora reads the notebook in full; pursues the planning-record
  thread to the approved inspector; the stairwell defect is confirmed
  in professional/technical terms; Pye's unexplained work surfaces and he
  begins to break under the weight of what he's avoided knowing; first
  direct confrontation between Nora and Cole where his control visibly
  costs him effort.
- **Part III**: Cass's fate is confirmed; the concealment in Flat 10 is
  discovered; Pye's reckoning; Cole's downfall engineered through the same
  public-record/regulatory mechanism Cass originally used — Nora finishes
  what Cass started, using the same skills, deliberately mirrored. Nora's
  Marcus subplot resolves on its own, separate timeline, before the
  climax — she has to have already decided who she trusts before the
  building forces the question.
