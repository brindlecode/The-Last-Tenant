## Chapter 1 — "Move-in Day"

**Time**: Mid-November, a Saturday. Story day 1.

**Scene list**:
1. Nora arrives at Sovereign Mill by car with the last of her boxes (most
   of the move happened days earlier via a van; this final trip is the one
   she does alone, deliberately, after Marcus offered to help and she said
   no). First sight of the building.
2. Lobby: meets Desmond Pye. The buzzer panel, "FERRIS" hastily taped over
   with "HALE."
3. Julian Cole appears personally to welcome her — unusual, charming,
   already knows small true things about her (from her tenancy
   application) that unsettle her slightly.
4. Alone in Flat 6: too clean, fresh paint smell in one spot, forwarded
   mail for C. Ferris on the mat. Nora's professional eye clocks the
   amateur plaster patch without yet knowing to care.
5. Night: unpacking, alone for the first time in the flat, finds the torn
   notebook-page corner wedged under loose skirting board.

**Story goal**: Establish Nora, her voice, her wound (via what she notices
and avoids), the building, Cole, and Pye — and plant the chapter's clues
without a single overt "mystery" beat until the very last page.

**Clues planted this chapter** (see `bible/03-mystery-architecture.md`
clue map): #1 (buzzer sticker), #2 (forwarded mail), #3 (plaster patch/
paint smell), #4 (notebook fragment, end of chapter), #5 begins
(brochure-vs-reality gap, light touch only).

**Character beats**: Nora's competence on display (reads the building
correctly) alongside her personal rawness (the single trip alone; not
answering Marcus's text right away). Cole's warmth-as-control introduced
at full strength but entirely deniable — nothing he does is anything other
than a good landlord's behavior, read twice. Pye understated, kind,
already visibly carrying something around Cass's name without saying so.

**Entry state**: Nora has just left the marital home behind; knows nothing
of Cass Ferris beyond a name on a lease clause.

**Exit state**: Nora has a physical object (the notebook fragment) she
doesn't yet understand the significance of, and a first, still-dismissible
prickle that something about this "great deal" of a flat doesn't add up.

**Prohibited this chapter**: All eight items in
`registers/prohibited-revelations.md`. In particular: nothing may suggest
Cole has ever met Cass beyond a landlord's ordinary knowledge of a tenant;
the plaster patch is noticed, never explained.

**Target length**: ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 2 — "Settling In"

**Time**: The following week, into the next weekend. Story days 2–8.

**Scene list**:
1. Weekday unpacking/settling montage-compressed: Nora's surveyor habits
   surface uninvited (checks the fire door self-closer on her own floor,
   counts escape routes without meaning to).
2. Meets Rena properly across the landing; first real conversation, warm,
   funny; the jumper detail; "Cass just sort of vanished, people do that."
3. Phone call with Marcus: paperwork, an old argument's residue, establishes
   the marriage's unfinished business and Nora's guilt/anger mix.
4. Nora explores the building's shared spaces (gym/laundry on 1st, roof
   terrace); finds the Plant Room door on 4, fresh scuff marks inconsistent
   with a quiet building.
5. Back in the flat: the smart-hub/resident portal is still logged into
   Cass's account. Nora means to log out for her; a notification preview
   surfaces first — enough to see "J.C." and "the report," not enough to
   read the message. She closes it without opening more.

**Story goal**: Deepen the building and cast (Rena, the Hoist vs. stair
texture, the portal), let Nora's competence keep doing the noticing work,
and give the reader a second, harder-to-explain-away anomaly (the portal
notification) to sit with alongside Chapter 1's fragment.

**Clues planted/reinforced**: #5 continues, #6 (Hoist vs. stair,
world-texture only, never flagged as significant), #7 (Plant Room scuff
marks), #10 (portal notification), #11 begins (Rena's anecdotes).

**Character beats**: Rena's easy warmth versus the guardedness Nora's
learned to keep around new people since Marcus. Nora's restraint
(logging out rather than reading on) matters — establishes her ethics
early, so later choices to dig read as escalation, not a static trait.

**Entry state**: Nora has an unexplained fragment and a faint unease.

**Exit state**: Two more anomalies she can't yet connect, and a first,
still-private admission to herself that she's curious in a way that isn't
only professional.

**Prohibited this chapter**: All items in `prohibited-revelations.md`. In
particular: the portal notification must show only "J.C." and "the report"
— no further message content, no confirmation of what "the report" is.

**Target length**: ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 3 — "The Dossier Thread"

**Time**: The following week. Story days 9–14.

**Scene list**:
1. At Hollis & Vane, using professional access with a thin but real
   pretext (curiosity framed to a colleague as "checking my own building,
   force of habit"), Nora pulls Sovereign Mill's planning and building-
   control file.
2. She finds the fast-tracked private-inspector sign-off, and — the real
   jolt — an old public objection during the original planning application,
   filed by "C. Ferris, freelance journalist," years before Cass ever
   became a tenant there.
3. Coffee with Priya at the Ropewalk Café: Nora tells her some but not all
   of it; Priya's "are you doing the thing again" lands and doesn't fully
   land — the novel keeps both readings alive.
4. Back at Sovereign Mill: Cole has left a handwritten note inviting her to
   a small welcome dinner with a couple of other residents. At the dinner
   (chapter's closing scene), Cole mentions Cass Ferris by name, unprompted,
   before Nora has ever said it to him.

**Story goal**: Move the mystery from "atmosphere and small objects" to a
real, checkable, public-record fact — Cass's interest in this building
predates her tenancy — while deepening Nora's isolation/self-doubt theme
through Priya, and closing on the chapter's sharpest character-level
wrongness: Cole knowing a name he was never given.

**Clues planted/reinforced**: #12 (planning record + pre-tenancy objection)
— central backbone clue, #5 reinforced with real stakes now visible, #13
(Cole names Cass unprompted, chapter's final beat).

**Character beats**: Nora's professional identity as her most trustworthy
self versus Priya's read of her personal unreliability — the chapter should
let both feel true. Cole at his most charming and most exposed in a single
small slip, written so it's genuinely deniable (he could plausibly have
seen the name on file, on the buzzer sticker, from Pye — the text should
not resolve which).

**Entry state**: Two physical anomalies, no throughline connecting them to
anything beyond "odd."

**Exit state**: A real, public-record fact connecting Cass to the building
before her tenancy, plus a live, specific unease about Cole himself for the
first time — not just the building.

**Prohibited this chapter**: All items in `prohibited-revelations.md`. In
particular: Cole's knowledge of Cass's name must remain explicable by
ordinary means (tenancy file, buzzer sticker, Pye) — never confirmed as
anything more.

**Target length**: ~2,700 words.

## Chapter 4 — "The Fourth Floor"

**Time**: Days after the dinner. Story days 15–16.

**Scene list**:
1. Morning after the dinner: Nora, unsettled, seeks out Pye directly under
   a plausible pretext (asking about a maintenance issue) and pushes,
   gently, about Cass.
2. Pye's slip: "She asked me the same thing you're asking. About the fourth
   floor." He immediately backs off, visibly regretting it, and won't say
   more.
3. Nora goes up to the fourth floor under another thin pretext (checking
   the Plant Room noise complaint she's decided to invent, or genuinely
   investigating the scuff marks from Ch.2). Flat 10's door is propped for
   "paint fumes"; a contractor is audible but briefly absent.
4. Through the doorway: a section of wall clearly more freshly finished
   than the rest of the "stalled renovation," painter's tape still down —
   and, among genuine offcuts and rubble bags near the threshold, Cass's
   notebook: burnt-orange cover, spiral bound, "C.F." in silver paint pen,
   unmistakably matching the torn corner from Chapter 1.
5. Nora takes it. Footsteps on the Hoist. She retreats to her own flat,
   notebook in hand, and the chapter ends on her locking her own door
   behind her for the first time since moving in.

**Story goal**: Convert accumulated unease into undeniable physical
evidence — the chapter's job is to be the button on the whole four-chapter
unit. Everything before this chapter could still be coincidence; this
chapter ends that possibility for Nora (and the reader) without resolving
what it means.

**Clues planted/reinforced**: #14 (Pye's slip, central spoken clue of the
unit), #7/#8 reinforced (scuff marks, missing log entry, used as her
pretext for going up), #15 (the notebook itself — the act-out).

**Character beats**: Pye's slip must cost him visibly — a physical
tell (he stops, looks at his own hands, changes the subject too fast) —
so it reads as a real breach of something he's held for months, not
convenient exposition. Nora's decision to take the notebook is an active,
morally weighted choice (this is a step past looking; it's closer to
trespass and theft, and the prose should let her feel that, not skip past
it) — consistent with her established restraint in Chapter 2, now
deliberately abandoned under greater cause.

**Entry state**: Suspicion, unease, no proof.

**Exit state**: Physical proof in hand that the "she just moved out" story
is false. Nora has crossed a line she chose to cross. Nothing about Cole's
guilt or Cass's fate is confirmed — only that the official story is a lie.

**Prohibited this chapter**: All items in `prohibited-revelations.md` remain
in force at the end of this chapter — this is the hardest chapter to keep
honest, because the temptation is to let Pye or the physical scene over-
explain. Confirm at audit: no character states or implies Cass is dead; no
character states or implies Cole caused her disappearance; the notebook is
found closed/unread beyond its cover in this chapter (its contents are a
Chapter 5+ matter).

**Target length**: ~2,900 words.
