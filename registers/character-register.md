# Character Register (living)

Baseline states below are drawn from `bible/02-characters.md`. Each chapter
that establishes new on-page detail (physical description used, habits
shown, knowledge gained) gets a dated append under "Chapter log." Audits
check new prose against the most recent state here, not just against
Gate 2.

## Baseline (pre-Chapter 1)

- **Nora Hale** — 34, chartered building surveyor, Associate at Hollis &
  Vane. Separated from Marcus, not divorced. Driving her own car (make/model
  not yet specified — first surveyor to specify if it comes up: keep
  unremarkable, practical). Knows nothing of Cass Ferris beyond a name on a
  lease clause.
- **Julian Cole** — 44, owner-developer, Sovereign Living. Knowledge-state:
  complete (see Gate 3). Not a POV character; only externally observable
  behavior may appear on the page.
- **Desmond Pye** — 61, caretaker, ground-floor flat. Knowledge-state:
  partial/avoided (see Gate 3).
- **Rena Okafor** — 31, illustrator, Flat 5. Knowledge-state: ordinary-
  neighbor level only.
- **Cass Ferris** — 28 at disappearance, freelance journalist, formerly
  Flat 6. Off-page throughout Part I; appears only via traces.
- **Marcus Hale** — 36, off-page throughout Ch.1–4; phone/text only.
- **Priya Chandrasekaran** — 35, family solicitor, Whitlock & Bryce. Off-
  site friend, phone/text/coffee only.

## Chapter log

*(Appended after each chapter is drafted and audited. Format: Chapter — new
established facts — register updated by.)*

- **Ch.1**: **Nora** — established on-page: drives herself, does the final
  move alone by choice (declined Marcus's help); professional habit of
  reading buildings is involuntary/constant, not switched on for the case;
  keeps an evidence bag in her site kit as ordinary professional kit, not
  as premeditated investigation; still not divorced, still guilty about
  the split, avoids Marcus's texts same-day. **Julian Cole** — established
  on-page: personally greets new tenants, already knows Nora's employer/
  profession from her tenancy application (explicable, on the record),
  physically takes control of tasks (the box) without waiting for
  agreement, comfortable saying "I'm effectively always in the building."
  No interiority accessed (correct per POV rule). **Desmond Pye** —
  established on-page: opens the door before she can knock (attentive/
  watchful), flatly confirms Cass as "previous tenant" without
  elaboration, mild deflection ("I keep meaning to [replace the label]")
  under a totally ordinary surface. **Rena, Priya, Cass, Marcus**: no
  on-page appearance yet (Marcus: phone text only, consistent with Gate 2
  — off-page for Ch.1–4).

- **Ch.2**: **Nora** — habits now extend beyond doors to counting exits
  and reading extinguisher tags; has adopted a daily Ropewalk Café routine
  as private, deliberate "leaving" time. Restrained a second time (logs
  Cass's portal out rather than reading on) — second data point for a
  consistent trait, not a one-off. **Rena Okafor** — fully introduced on
  page: illustrator, Flat 5, two years in the building, warm/talkative,
  wears Cass's old grey jumper (moth hole, underarm), rents by choice not
  necessity, calls Cole "Julian," reports he personally fixed her
  radiator on a Sunday. Her account of Cass's departure (sudden "change of
  plans" text, asked her to watch some boxes, went silent, boxes later
  cleared by Cole) is now the fullest secondhand version on record —
  matches Gate 3's fake-departure narrative exactly, from a source with no
  reason to doubt it. **Marcus** — first live dialogue (phone call): texts
  before calling now; used "I just want this to be easy, for both of us,"
  unknowingly echoing his own remortgage-era phrasing (Gate 2 backstory) —
  Nora notices the echo, he doesn't. **Cass (backstory, via Rena)** — also
  had the fire-door-testing habit; was "a bit stressed, moaning about
  work" in her final weeks; mentioned "a stupid thing about the stairs" as
  a work matter and laughed it off.

- **Ch.3**: **Nora** — corrected to twelve years professional experience
  (see continuity register Fixed Numbers). **Julian Cole** — hosts
  personally and well; tells a self-deprecating building-renovation story
  in company; names Cass unprompted, unhedged, mid-sentence, and shows a
  single, brief, non-verbal reaction when Nora says the name back to him —
  no interiority, no confirmation either way. Has three completed Rookford
  conversions plus one live, mentioned but not yet investigated.
  **Desmond Pye** — a second, larger slip than Ch.1's flat deflection: "She
  had a lot of post, near the end. More than most," immediately
  followed by visible self-correction and retreat into small talk —
  matches his Gate 2 pattern of saying more than he means to under mild
  pressure. **Rena Okafor** — on-page at the dinner, comfortable/regular
  presence in Cole's flat, knows his stories already. **Priya
  Chandrasekaran** — first on-page appearance: solicitor's directness,
  "lawyer face," calls Nora "Nor," knows the Marcus/joint-statements
  history in specific detail, shows affection through small physical
  gestures (stealing food, a held-out appraising look) more than words.
  **Cass (backstory)**: confirmed freelance affiliation, **Rookford
  Courier**; confirmed to have filed a public planning objection about
  Sovereign Mill's fire strategy three years before Nora ever heard the
  building's name (the prose's exact anchor point — see
  `timeline-register.md`'s backstory entry, corrected to "~3 years before
  story start" during the combined four-chapter audit to match this).

- **Ch.4**: **Nora** — crosses a real line for the first time: takes
  physical evidence rather than only looking/reading. Immediately
  self-regulates around it (won't open the notebook without more
  deliberation; explicitly defers reading it for "several days" beyond
  the chapter's end) — escalation with a still-intact ethical governor,
  not a character break. Starts using her own deadbolt. Explicitly
  resolves (interiority, tied to the Marcus/remortgage wound) not to be
  "the last person in the room to be told" again — this is the chapter
  that turns her personal wound into her stated motive for the
  investigation, on the page, for the first time. **Desmond Pye** — his
  largest slip yet, visibly costly (face goes "old, and tired, and
  briefly unguarded" for a matter of seconds before he shuts back down
  into deflection); confirms Cass asked him almost the identical question
  Nora just asked, specifically about the fourth floor. **Cass
  (backstory)** — notebook physically recovered and confirmed hers beyond
  doubt (cover detail, matched tear); her fate remains completely
  unconfirmed on the page, by design.
