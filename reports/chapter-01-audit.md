# Chapter 1 Audit Report — "Move-in Day"

## Critique (pre-revision)

First full draft hit all five scenes in the Gate 5 brief and landed the
voice, but a re-read surfaced three concrete craft problems rather than
vague unease, which is the standard this pass is held to:

1. **Repetition tic**: "the way she ___" constructions appeared three
   times across the chapter — close enough together in a short piece to
   read as a mannerism rather than a rhythm.
2. **"Eight months" repeated verbatim three times** (tenancy change, post
   records, notebook age) — reinforces the timeline usefully but as
   identical phrasing it reads as carelessness, not motif.
3. **Three consecutive paragraphs opening with "She ___"** at the chapter's
   close (the notebook-fragment discovery), where the rest of the chapter
   otherwise varies its openings deliberately.

A fourth item was considered and *not* changed: the opening sentence leads
with a reference to rain/photographs before the first embodied action.
This technically brushes against the style guide's "open in scene, not
reflection or weather" rule, but the very next sentence is Nora physically
parking the car — judged in-scene overall and voice-establishing enough to
keep. Recorded here rather than silently overridden.

## Revision

- Varied the second "the way she ___" instance (patch-noticing beat) to
  "filed it away rather than flagging it — that was how she handled most
  things these days," which also strengthens the deliberate "filed, not
  flagged" motif (now used twice, purposefully, not by accident) with the
  earlier instance at the Pye/buzzer-panel beat.
- Reworded the notebook-fragment age reference to "older than the tenancy
  file made this flat's history sound," removing the third verbatim "eight
  months" while keeping the two remaining instances (tenancy record; post
  record), which now read as two independent, corroborating data points
  rather than a repeated tic.
- Rewrote the closing paragraph's opening from "She put it in the bag
  anyway" to "In it went anyway," breaking the three-in-a-row "She"
  openings while preserving the terse, deliberate rhythm of the two that
  remain ("She should put it in the bin." / "She got up instead...").

## Six audits

### Continuity
Checked against `registers/continuity-register.md` and
`bible/01-world-and-setting.md`. **PASS.** Building geography, names, and
object details are all consistent with the bible. One planning-vs-draft
mismatch found and resolved by updating the register to match the prose
(the correct direction for a living register): the plaster patch was
planned at "chest-height," drafted at "shoulder height" — register now
reads shoulder-height as ground truth. One deliberate improvement over
plan: the notebook fragment is now found on the *same* wall as the plaster
patch (Nora's chosen desk wall), tightening the clue cluster and matching
Gate 3's backstory detail that this was Cass's desk wall too — logged in
the continuity register. One open item carried to Chapter 2: Flat 6's
floor number (2nd) was never stated outright on the page; not an error,
but flagged for Chapter 2 to state plainly.

### Clue
Checked against `bible/03-mystery-architecture.md` clue map and
`registers/clue-register.md`. **PASS.** Clues #1–4 fully planted as
designed; #5 planted at the intended light touch. Fair-play holds: every
clue is something Nora physically finds or is told with no motive to
deceive attached to the teller (Pye's flat, factual line about a "previous
tenant"). Nothing over-explained — the patch and fragment are noticed and
filed, never interpreted by the narration. Red herring status correct:
Marcus appears only as emotional texture (guilt, unanswered text), no
evidentiary weight attached, per plan.

### Voice
Checked against `registers/voice-style-guide.md`. **PASS.** Close third
past tense held throughout; no head-hopping; Cole and Pye rendered
entirely externally. British usage consistent (recognised, judgement,
manoeuvring, grey). Two of the guide's recurring motifs deployed
deliberately: "noticing vs. saying" (filed/flagged, twice) and "doors and
closers" (lobby fire door; her own flat's correctly-specified front door).
Banned stock phrases: none present. One forward-tracking note: the
building's goods lift is deliberately kept lowercase/unnamed in Nora's
own perception this chapter (she's too new to know building slang);
logged in the continuity register as a planned Chapter 2 beat — a resident
names it "the Hoist" on the page, after which the proper noun capitalizes.

### Pacing
Checked against the Gate 5 scene list. **PASS.** All five briefed scenes
present, in order, proportionate (roughly 500/350/450/600/750 words), with
the discovery scene correctly weighted as the chapter's climax. The Marcus
text-message beat is placed as a tension-modulating breath immediately
before the fragment discovery, which is a stronger position than the brief
specified and was kept. Chapter opens in scene and closes on an unresolved
image (lying awake, unable to tell which sounds are ordinary), matching
the style guide's chapter-boundary discipline.

### Factual
Checked surveying/construction/property terminology and general
plausibility. **PASS.** "Intumescent strip," "self-closer," "party wall,"
"raking light," "snagging list," and "chartered surveyor" are all used
correctly and in period/context. Converted-mill features (retained cast
iron columns, exposed hoist beam, re-motorized goods lift with a lattice
gate) are standard, real features of UK heritage residential conversions —
plausible without needing invented technical over-explanation. No real
place names, brands, or regulatory bodies used. Royal Mail forwarding
lapsing for one sender's records after eight months is realistic. No
factual claims made about fire-safety regulation specifics (correctly
deferred — that's Chapter 3+ territory and is not attempted here).

### Prose
Line-level pass, post-revision. **PASS.** The three repetition issues
found in critique are resolved (see Revision above). Sentence rhythm
varies deliberately between long qualifying sentences and short blunt ones
("A patch." / "She should put it in the bin."). No cliché or filler
stock phrases. One item carried forward as a watch item rather than a
defect: em dash frequency is high (intentional — it's load-bearing for
Nora's parenthetical, self-correcting interiority) and should be monitored
across Chapters 2–4 so it doesn't calcify into a tic once the novelty of
the voice wears off.
