# Chapter 3 Audit Report — "The Dossier Thread"

## Critique (pre-revision)

First draft came in at 1,823 words against a 2,700 target, the largest
gap of the unit so far. Beyond length, re-reading surfaced two real
problems and one subtler one:

1. **A dropped narrative thread**: an early version had Nora "going home"
   mid-scene, before she'd actually reached the planning file or found
   Cass's objection — a sequencing error introduced while drafting an
   addition, not present in the original outline. Caught before it
   reached final text.
2. **A duplicated fixed number**: "eleven years" was being used both for
   Nora's marriage (correct, locked in `bible/02-characters.md`) and,
   separately and coincidentally, for her professional experience
   (not locked anywhere — an invented detail that happened to reuse the
   same number). Four instances across Chapters 1 and 3 used "eleven
   years in the trade" / "eleven years of practice" / "eleven years being
   paid to notice" for her career. Left alone, an attentive reader could
   read significance into two unrelated facts sharing an identical,
   specific number.
3. Underweight scenes, same category of issue as Chapter 2: the Priya
   scene and the dinner scene both had room for real material the brief
   implied but the first pass hadn't earned yet (Priya's specific
   objection; the dinner's texture as an actual small gathering, not just
   a two-hander with a reveal line bolted on).

## Revision

- Fixed the sequencing error: moved the "restrains herself from checking
  Cole's other developments" beat to after the objection discovery
  (where it belongs causally) instead of before the planning file had
  even been reached.
- Fixed the number collision: changed all four career-experience
  instances (one in Chapter 1, three in Chapter 3) from "eleven" to
  "twelve" years, and logged the distinction explicitly in
  `registers/continuity-register.md` under a new "Fixed numbers" section
  so it doesn't drift back together in Chapters 4+.
- Expanded on merit: a sharper, more specific exchange with Priya (Nora
  defending the file's substance directly, Priya naming the "unpacking a
  marriage" fear plainly rather than staying oblique) and a closing beat
  on the cobbles that gives their friendship an actual moment rather than
  ending on the joke about flapjacks; a second, larger Pye slip ("she had
  a lot of post, near the end") that both deepens his Gate 2 pattern and
  plants a small, safe new detail; more of the dinner as a populated
  scene (a Cole anecdote landing with the room, two lightly-sketched
  other guests) before the chapter's central beat.
- Also fixed, while re-reading the expansion: Pye's post comment could
  have been misread as implying he'd done concealment work himself if
  phrased carelessly — checked against `registers/prohibited-revelations.md`
  item 4 and confirmed the final wording ("she had a lot of post, near
  the end") stays strictly about correspondence volume, not construction.

Post-revision word count: 2,411 (prose only, excluding heading).

## Six audits

### Continuity
Checked against `registers/continuity-register.md` and
`bible/01-world-and-setting.md`. **PASS**, with the eleven/twelve-years
fix as the notable finding (see above; corrected, not just flagged). The
approved inspector's name is used identically in the building-control
file and on the envelope's return address — deliberate cross-corroboration,
matches the plan exactly. The Loft's description is new and consistent
with Gate 1 (top floor, unnumbered). Two dinner guests are named but
explicitly hedged as uncertain in Nora's own perception — logged as
soft canon rather than hard fact, so a later chapter is free to handle
them loosely without contradiction.

### Clue
Checked against `bible/03-mystery-architecture.md` and
`registers/clue-register.md`. **PASS.** Clue #12 (the pre-tenancy
objection) is the strongest backbone clue landed so far — entirely public
record, entirely something Nora is professionally positioned to find,
zero coincidence beyond the premise itself. Clue #13 (Cole naming Cass
unprompted) is correctly built with multiple live, ordinary explanations
and a reaction described only physically ("something moved behind his
face"), never interpreted by the narration. Clue #9 (the envelope) is
planted and stays sealed. Clue #5 gets real documentary weight for the
first time without tipping into an accusation.

### Voice
Checked against `registers/voice-style-guide.md`. **PASS.** Cole remains
externally observed throughout, including at his most extended on-page
appearance yet (the dinner) — every charming or unsettling beat is
something Nora sees or hears, never his interiority. Priya's introduction
gives the chapter a second distinct spoken voice (direct, unsentimental,
legally precise) that reads as clearly different from Rena's warmth or
Pye's evasiveness — checked against Gate 2 and holds up. "Noticing vs.
saying" recurs at the chapter's climax ("She didn't let it go. She simply
didn't say so out loud") — the clearest, highest-stakes use of the motif
yet, appropriately, since this is the chapter where the pattern starts
costing her something.

### Pacing
Checked against the Gate 5 scene list. **PASS.** All four briefed beats
present (records pull, coffee, note, dinner) with the envelope woven in
as a same-day coda to the first rather than a separate scene, which
tightens rather than pads the chapter. The dinner is the longest,
best-earned scene in the unit so far, correctly weighted as the chapter's
(and arguably the four-chapter unit's) centerpiece. Ending avoids
repeating Chapters 1 and 2's identical "lying awake" closing beat —
deliberate variation — while still landing on forward pressure ("She
wasn't going to stop").

### Factual
**PASS.** UK planning-portal mechanics (public comments, conditions,
approved inspectors vs. council building control) are represented
accurately and without over-explaining. "EPC rating" as a casual
comparison is period- and profession-accurate shorthand. No real
regulators, publications, or firms used — Rookford Building Control
Partnership and the Rookford Courier are both confirmed invented per
`bible/01-world-and-setting.md`'s rules.

### Prose
**PASS**, post-revision. The number-collision fix was the main structural
finding; line-level read otherwise clean. Dialogue carries two
distinguishable new voices (Priya, and Cole at greater length than
previous chapters) without either slipping into the other's register or
into Nora's own. No banned stock phrases. Em dash frequency continues
the moderate level established in Chapter 2, not creeping back up to
Chapter 1's density — the watch item from Chapter 1 is now resolved
across three chapters of evidence, not just one.

## Addendum (found during Chapter 4 drafting, fixed retroactively)

While drafting Chapter 4's near-miss checks (see that chapter's audit),
a related risk was spotted in this chapter on re-read: the Loft's roof
trusses were originally described as looking like "the ribcage of
something very large that had died gently." Not a factual leak — it's
architectural metaphor, not a claim about Cass — but landing that
specific word in Cole's own flat, of all places, was more thematic
foreshadowing than the scene needed or the prohibited-revelations
discipline should risk. Changed to "the ribcage of something enormous
that had once, a very long time ago, been a forest," which keeps the
image (and ties it to the mill's history) without the loaded word. Noted
here rather than silently patched, since it's the same category of
near-miss flagged in Chapter 4 and the pattern is worth tracking across
the whole unit.
