# Chapter 4 Audit Report — "The Fourth Floor"

This is the four-chapter unit's climax and required the most careful
pass, both in craft terms (it's the act-out the whole unit builds to) and
in prohibited-revelations terms (it's the chapter where Nora gets closest
to the truth, which is exactly where accidental over-confirmation is most
likely). Treat this report as the most load-bearing of the four.

## Critique (pre-revision)

First full pass ran to roughly 2,000 words against a 2,900 target and,
more importantly, surfaced four real problems on close re-read — more
than any other chapter in the unit, which tracks with this being the
hardest chapter to get right:

1. **A direct near-miss on Item 1** (the single most important entry in
   `registers/prohibited-revelations.md`): the closing beat originally
   read "...heart still going, a dead woman's — *a missing woman's*, she
   made herself think instead..." — syntactically clumsy (the correction
   was buried mid-clause) and, worse, it repeated "a missing woman's" a
   second time later in the same sentence, which was simultaneously a
   prose repetition problem and a sign the fix had been bolted on rather
   than actually resolved.
2. **A second, unrelated instance of the same pattern**, introduced while
   fixing the first: a new line about "standing in a dead man's — a
   stranger's — wrecked room" that didn't even make sense (no "dead man"
   exists anywhere in this story; Flat 10 isn't associated with any male
   victim). This was a drafting slip, not a considered risk — caught on
   the same re-read as (1).
3. **A POV/plausibility break** in the escape sequence: Nora, hiding a
   full floor below Flat 10 behind a closed fire door, was described
   knowing that the returning contractor "didn't pause outside Flat 10" —
   something she has no way to perceive from where she's hiding. A
   believability error, not a revelation risk, but the kind of thing the
   Continuity/Factual audits exist to catch.
4. Underweight scenes, as with every chapter this unit: the "waiting an
   hour" beat and the room-description beat both had room for real
   material — tension and professional detail respectively — that the
   first pass hadn't yet earned.

A full-manuscript sweep (`grep` for death-adjacent words across all four
chapters, not just this one) was run specifically because of finding
(1) — see Registers Updated, below. It caught a fifth, lower-stakes
instance sitting in Chapter 3 since that chapter's own audit: the Loft's
roof trusses originally described as "the ribcage of something very
large that had died gently." Not a factual leak on its own terms, but the
same category of risk, landing in Cole's own flat specifically, and
removed on the same principle.

## Revision

- Rewrote the closing beat entirely: separated "the notebook" from any
  loaded adjective at first mention, then staged *Dead* as a single,
  clearly-marked word Nora's mind reaches for and explicitly sets back
  down as "unproven," corrected to *Missing* — tied explicitly to her
  professional standard of evidence ("the way she'd have made a junior
  colleague strike an unsupported conclusion from a report"). This is a
  considered piece of characterization now, not a hedge bolted onto a
  risky line: it dramatizes the exact discipline the prohibited-revelations
  system requires of the whole manuscript, inside one character's own
  head, which is a stronger and safer choice than simply avoiding the
  subject.
- Rewrote the "dead man's room" line to plain, unloaded language
  ("somebody else's wrecked, half-finished room").
- Rewrote the escape sequence so Nora only ever reports what she can
  actually perceive (footsteps, their pace, a door closing somewhere
  above), with an explicit "she assumed, though by then she was already
  moving and not inclined to stop and confirm it" — turns a plausibility
  hole into a small, honest character beat about her own adrenaline
  instead.
- Fixed Chapter 3's "died gently" line retroactively (see that chapter's
  audit addendum).
- Removed a specific "two days before" callback to Pye carrying bulbs
  (Chapter 3) that, on checking the timeline register's day-count, didn't
  actually line up with the intervening day count — rather than do the
  arithmetic gymnastics to force it to fit, generalized the line so it no
  longer makes a checkable claim.
- Expanded on merit: a fuller waking-up beat (a drafted-then-deleted text
  to Priya, showing the isolation pattern continuing); a fuller
  "waiting an hour" beat that does real character work (weighing Priya's
  "same shape" accusation against the specific, public, checkable facts
  she now has, and choosing to act regardless of which of them is right);
  more professional detail in the Flat 10 room description, specifically
  establishing the room reads as genuinely stalled and undisturbed rather
  than staged — important both for realism and so the notebook's presence
  reads as an accident the antagonist made, not a plant; a full additional
  suspense beat (the fire-door near-miss) that also reinforces the
  "doors and closers" motif at the chapter's highest-tension moment; an
  expanded closing passage tying the discovery back to the Marcus/
  remortgage wound explicitly, on the page, as her stated reason for not
  backing down.

Post-revision word count: 2,603 (prose only, excluding heading).

## Six audits

### Continuity
Checked against `registers/continuity-register.md` and prior chapters.
**PASS**, after the fixes above. The cold snap's arrival matches the
timeline register's Days 15–16 forecast exactly ("hard and dry"). The
notebook-to-fragment match is now framed as a confirmed pair in the
object tracker, not just a visual echo. New behavioral detail (the
deadbolt) logged as a trackable change for future chapters rather than a
one-off image. "Twelve years" (not "eleven") used correctly, consistent
with the Chapter 3 fix.

### Clue
Checked against `bible/03-mystery-architecture.md` and
`registers/clue-register.md`. **PASS.** Clues #14 and #15 — the two most
important payoffs in the entire four-chapter unit — both land cleanly.
#14 costs Pye something visible on the page, per Gate 5's explicit
requirement, rather than reading as convenient exposition. #15 is reached
through Nora's own established skills and choices (the pretext, the
professional reading of the room, the physical match to Chapter 1's
fragment) with no unearned luck beyond the premise that she lives in
Flat 6 at all. Clues #7/#8 are correctly reused as her actual in-scene
justification for going upstairs rather than left stranded as isolated
Chapter 2 texture.

### Voice
Checked against `registers/voice-style-guide.md`. **PASS.** Close third
held under the highest pressure it's faced yet — the temptation to let
the prose editorialize about what's "really" going on is strongest here,
and the final draft resists it throughout (the notebook proves the
official story is a lie; the narration never states or implies what the
true story is). The "noticing vs. saying" and "doors and closers" motifs
both recur at their highest stakes yet, which is correct chapter-4
placement for a Part-I-opening climax rather than overuse.

### Pacing
Checked against the Gate 5 scene list. **PASS.** All five briefed beats
present, correctly weighted — the Flat 10 sequence is, appropriately, the
longest and slowest-built scene in the entire four-chapter unit, with the
added near-miss earning its place as tension rather than padding. Ends on
a strong, non-repeating image (the deadbolt; the notebook set aside,
deliberately unread) that both closes this chapter and sets up work for
Chapter 5 without trespassing on it.

### Factual
**PASS**, after the escape-sequence fix. Fire door/self-closer behavior
is used correctly and consistently with Chapters 1–2's established
detail. A propped door under hazard tape with a chain latch, a
stalled-but-genuinely-messy building site, and a contractor's brief
absence are all ordinary, plausible site conditions — nothing here
requires the antagonist to have behaved carelessly beyond the one
established loose thread the mystery architecture already accounts for
(the misplaced notebook, per Gate 3).

### Prose
**PASS**, after the two prohibited-revelations-adjacent lines were
rewritten (see Critique/Revision above) — these were craft problems as
much as risk problems, and the fixes improved the writing on its own
terms, not just its safety. No banned stock phrases. Sentence rhythm
holds up under the chapter's sustained tension without tipping into
short-sentence overuse; the longer, qualifying sentences (e.g., the room
description, the closing passage) are used deliberately to slow the
reader down at exactly the moments the chapter needs breathing room.
