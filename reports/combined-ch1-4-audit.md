# Combined Audit — Part I, Chapters 1–4

This audit checks the four-chapter unit as a whole, on top of (not
instead of) each chapter's individual audit
(`reports/chapter-01-audit.md` through `chapter-04-audit.md`). Per-chapter
audits check a chapter against the registers as they stood at the time;
this pass checks the four chapters against *each other*, and re-checks
the registers for drift that accumulated across the unit.

## Method

In addition to a full re-read, an independent subagent was dispatched
with no prior context on this project and given: the confidential
solution (`bible/03-mystery-architecture.md`), the prohibited-revelations
list, the character/continuity/timeline/clue registers, and the four
manuscript chapters — and asked to adversarially hunt for prohibited-
revelation leaks, POV violations, cross-chapter continuity errors, and
repeated phrasing, plus give an overall craft assessment. Using a second,
context-free reader for this checkpoint specifically (rather than only
re-reading my own work again) was deliberate: by Chapter 4 I had been
close to this material long enough that a fresh, adversarial pass was
likely to catch things a fifth self-review would rationalize past. It
did — see Findings.

## Findings and resolution

### A) Prohibited revelations — no FAIL, two passages flagged for extra scrutiny, both held

All eight standing items were re-checked line by line across all four
chapters. No confirmed leak. Two passages were flagged as the tightest
margins in the manuscript and given extra scrutiny:

- Chapter 4's closing "*Dead* was the word that wanted to come next... she
  made herself put it back down, unproven... *Missing*" — independently
  assessed as dramatizing Nora's discipline rather than the narrator
  confirming a fact. Held as written.
- Chapter 4's Flat 10 description ("It was the only complete thing in the
  room") — flagged because the finished wall section maps precisely onto
  the actual concealment mechanism in the confidential solution. On
  review, this is correct and necessary: the wall has to be noticeably
  "the only complete thing in the room" for the *notebook* discovery
  (the sanctioned Chapter 4 reveal) to make sense, and Nora's attention
  is never directed at the wall as a place a body could be — only at
  what's sitting in front of it. Held as written. Flagging this
  explicitly here because it's the single detail in the unit doing the
  most double duty between "fair Chapter 4 clue" and "true Part III
  mechanism," and it deserves a second look at the start of Part II
  drafting to confirm it's still being used only as much as it's earned.

Full per-chapter sign-off remains in `registers/prohibited-revelations.md`.

### B) POV discipline — one real fix made

The audit found one line in Chapter 1 that stated Cole's internal
motivation as narrated fact rather than Nora's inference — a real
violation of the style guide's absolute (not just general) rule that
"Cole's interiority is never accessed." Fixed: "the specific, focused
warmth of a man who'd decided, at some point, professionally, that eye
contact was underused" → "a specific, focused warmth she clocked
immediately and couldn't help reading as the manner of a man who'd worked
out, somewhere along the way, that eye contact was underused" — now
explicitly framed as Nora's act of reading him, matching the technique
used correctly elsewhere for Cole (e.g., Chapter 3's "something moved
behind his face"). Several similar constructions for Marcus, Priya, and
Pye were also flagged but are within the style guide's general "infer"
allowance for non-Cole characters and were left as-is.

### C) Cross-chapter continuity — three real errors found and fixed

1. **Chapter 4 said "ordinary Tuesday things"** for a scene that opens
   the morning after Chapter 3's Friday dinner — should be Saturday.
   Fixed in the manuscript.
2. **A stale planning note in `timeline-register.md`** claimed "the
   first properly still, clear-skied cold night" fell around Day 6, but
   Chapter 2's actual prose never dramatizes that scene, and Chapter 3
   independently states the Day 14 dinner is "the first properly cold
   [night] since she'd moved in" — the two can't both be true. This was a
   register-vs-register problem (planning note vs. what was actually
   drafted), not a manuscript contradiction, but left uncorrected it
   would have become one the next time someone trusted the register.
   Fixed by removing the stale Day 6 claim.
3. **Cass's objection-filing date** was drafted as "three years before"
   in Chapter 3 prose and independently logged the same way in
   `registers/character-register.md`, but `timeline-register.md`'s
   original backstory entry said "~2 years before story start" — roughly
   a two-year gap between the planning-time figure and what actually got
   written and cross-confirmed twice. Fixed by correcting the timeline
   register to "~3 years before story start" to match the two
   independently-agreeing sources, on the principle that a living
   register updates to match confirmed prose, not the reverse.

All three are documented in full, with reasoning, at the point of the fix
in `registers/timeline-register.md` and `registers/character-register.md`
rather than silently corrected.

Also re-confirmed clean: every name/spelling across all four chapters
(zero misspellings found via full-text search); the eleven-years-married
vs. twelve-years-professional distinction (fully consistent in all six
instances across the unit); the Hoist capitalization convention; "eight
months since Cass" stated consistently; the notebook/fragment physical
match; Chapter 2's day-count arithmetic.

### D) Cross-chapter prose repetition — three items addressed

1. **"A beat, [physical detail]."** used as an identical sentence shape,
   for the identical character (Pye) and identical dramatic function
   (marking a hesitation before a near-slip), in consecutive chapters
   (3 and 4) — the tightest repetition found in the unit. Varied the
   Chapter 4 instance.
2. **"Without deciding to"** appeared three times verbatim, two of them
   only twelve lines apart within Chapter 4 itself. Varied the more
   minor of the two Chapter 4 instances ("She counted the offcuts without
   deciding to" → "on pure reflex"), keeping the more load-bearing use.
3. **"The building's ordinary sounds"** bookends Chapters 1, 2, and 4 —
   assessed as a deliberate, well-built structural motif (each use
   measurably advances what Nora knows relative to the last one) rather
   than a problem, and is now formally documented in
   `registers/voice-style-guide.md` so it reads as intentional design
   rather than an unreviewed accident. The related "filed [away]" motif
   (Ch.1 ×2, Ch.3 ×1) was similarly reviewed and documented rather than
   cut, for the same reason.

### E) Overall craft assessment (independent reader's judgment, endorsed)

The unit reads as a well-constructed opening: Nora's surveyor lens gives
the prose a consistent, distinctive engine for dread through physical
specifics rather than mood adjectives, and the fair-play contract is
respected throughout. Chapter 3 (the dinner) is the strongest single
chapter. Chapter 4 earns its climax, with one fair note: its danger is
procedural/ethical (the line Nora crosses by taking evidence) rather than
physical, which is consistent with the book's deliberately quiet register
but slightly undersells the visceral charge a commercial-suspense reader
might expect from a Part I climax — noted for awareness in Part II
rather than acted on here, since it's a register choice, not a defect.
Chapter 2 is the comparatively weakest of the four — four loosely-joined
scenes rather than one throughline, and its "By Monday / By Tuesday / By
Wednesday" opening verges on mechanical — but it lands its clues cleanly
and was not judged to need rework; noted as a pattern to watch rather
than a blocking issue for this unit.

## Verdict

All findings from both the per-chapter audits and this combined pass have
been resolved in the manuscript and registers, not deferred. No
prohibited revelation survives in the text. The one real POV violation
and the three real continuity errors found by the independent review are
fixed. The four chapters are internally consistent with each other and
with the registers as of this pass.

**The four-chapter unit is coherent and audited. Cleared to commit.**
